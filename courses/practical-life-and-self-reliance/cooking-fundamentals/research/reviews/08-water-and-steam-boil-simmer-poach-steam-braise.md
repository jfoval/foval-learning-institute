### Findings

**Serious**

- **S1. Meat-free braise and exercise allowed "white beans" held at a simmer.** White cannellini are
  white kidney beans, which the FDA's Bad Bug Book chapter (read in full, SOURCES B 5.1) says carry
  about a third of red kidney beans' toxin, and the braise holds the pot at 82 to 88°C, below the
  boil the FDA asks for, in the range its slow-cooker casseroles failed at. The lesson only fenced off
  red kidney beans. Fix: chickpeas only, with kidney beans of any colour named as excluded and the
  reason given.

**Moderate**

- **F1. "Tough meat, and why time does what heat can't" and the misconception "A harder boil cooks a
  braise faster" asserted, in effect, that heat does not speed collagen breakdown.** The gate note
  itself said no source here says either way, and the lesson's own "neither says" sentence agreed,
  but the heading, the misconception entry ("It adds a few degrees") and quiz item 3's explanation
  ("without speeding anything the course has a source for") leaned on the absence. Purslow 2005
  (abstract, read at this review) says connective tissue's contribution falls "at higher temperatures
  and longer cooking times". Fix: heading, misconception, predict answer and quiz explanation
  reworded to say no source quantifies the trade; "a few degrees" corrected to 12 to 18°C, which is
  what the lesson says elsewhere.
- **F2. Unsourced mechanistic claim in the chicken checkpoint**: "She gains less speed than she
  thinks ... the time is set mostly by how fast heat conducts to the centre". By conduction theory a
  100°C bath brings a centre to 74°C substantially faster than an 80°C one (roughly 1.5 to 2 times
  for a slab, since the centre approaches the bath temperature exponentially). Fix: says she does gain
  speed, and pays for it in the outer layers.
- **F3. Steam "at least as fast as boiling water" from overlapping ranges** (3,000 to 100,000
  against 5,000 to 100,000). Overlapping ranges do not support "at least". Fix: "roughly the rate",
  with both ranges printed.
- **F4. Unsourced claim that tough cuts carry more connective tissue, mostly collagen** (drafter's
  flag 1). Closed with Purslow 2005, abstract, direct (appended to SOURCES as "Stage 4 note, lesson
  8"): amount varies with muscle position and animal age and "has long been recognised to influence
  the tenderness of cooked meat". The naming of shoulder, shin and cheek stays as general knowledge
  and is labelled so in footnote 19. "Hard-working" dropped, since no source read gives the reason.
- **F5. Poaching at 75 to 80°C** (drafter's inference 1). Labelled, but at 75°C the centre of the
  breast creeps asymptotically towards the water and the 73.9°C US figure is barely below it, so a
  reader following the low end waits a long time. Fix: about 80°C, with the reason stated and
  labelled as this course's reading.

**Minor**

- **M1.** "It takes about two hours of simmering to get below 10%": the 2-hour row is 10%, not below.
  Fixed to "down to 10%".
- **M2.** Latorre: "They also found that some of the collagen gives way easily and some resists";
  the abstract says the results "support the hypothesis". Fixed ("Their results, they write, support
  the idea"). The dropped qualifier on strength ("although not as markedly as the Warner-Bratzler
  peak force measure of toughness") does not bear on the lesson's point; left.
- **M3.** USU paraphrase "matters little for cooking" widened the source's scope ("This isn't a
  problem when cooking a roast or chicken"). Fixed to the source's scope (defect 19).
- **M4.** "A Chinese red-braised pork belly, a West African groundnut stew and a Mexican pot of beans
  are other braises built the same way" (drafter's flag 1). Not every braise browns and deglazes, and
  a pot of beans is not built that way (and brings in the bean question). Fixed: two dishes named as
  long, gentle cooking in a covered pot, "each with its own order of steps". Hainanese chicken rice
  (poached bird, rice cooked in the broth) and Cantonese steamed fish on a plate are accurate at the
  level stated and the lesson disclaims authority on the dish; acceptable without a source.
- **M5.** Thawing: "Lesson 4 gave the safe ways to thaw: fridge, cold water, microwave" is the US
  list; lesson 4 taught that the FSA leaves out cold water. Fixed to give both.
- **M6.** EPA footnote read level "full, directly"; the gate closure records the answer read direct,
  not the whole page. Fixed to "the answer, directly".
- **M7.** "if you're home, finish it another way at once" and the reheating methods were not in
  SOURCES' record, though the page was read in full. Verified on the Wayback capture and recorded in
  the SOURCES note; lesson now gives FSIS's options.
- **M8.** Cold start: the exercise required "a recipe you own that uses alcohol". A reader may not
  have one. Fixed to any recipe that cooks with wine, beer or spirits.
- **M9.** Exercise step 2 boils the braise to read a boil temperature, which the lesson says toughens
  and dries meat. Kept (the reading is worth having), limited to a minute or two.
