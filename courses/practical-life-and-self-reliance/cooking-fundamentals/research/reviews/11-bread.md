### Findings

**Serious**

- **F1. The flavour quotation was turned into a shape claim.** "López-Alt found that the original
  slow no-knead loaf's crumb 'fell flat in every sample'". ATK wrote "the flavor of the crumb fell
  flat in every sample". In a bread lesson that has just discussed flat loaves, the trimmed lead-in
  reads as the crumb collapsing. Defect 19 (the sentence before the quotation). Fix: quote the whole
  clause.
- **F2. The final checkpoint attributed a claim to the yeast page that it does not make.** "The
  yeast is high, which ... is also what King Arthur's yeast page says costs flavour." The page ties
  the flavour cost to dough temperature (86 to 95°F), not to the amount of yeast. The same slide, in
  softer form, in misconception 2 ("fast, warm fermentation"). Fix: attribute temperature to the
  page and label the step to quantity as the course's reading, in both places.
- **F3. The two countries do not say the same thing about play dough.** "Both countries agree
  here ... the CDC's page covers play dough and crafts, and so does the FSA's." The CDC's "Do not"
  list includes "Let children play with or eat dough made with raw flour", crafts included; the FSA
  gives handling advice "to support how you manage the risks" of crafts and play and does not say
  not to. They agree on eating; they differ in strength on play. Attributing a US rule to both
  countries is the error this lesson was told to check for. Fix: state the difference.

**Moderate**

- **F4. The flat-loaf diagnosis merged two causes and missed the rival reading of "dense".** "The
  oven not hot, and the centre not done ... Either would explain the gummy middle." A cold pot
  (Philip's "hot pot makes the bread jump") predicts less spring, which is flatness, not gumminess.
  And the recipe itself says dough left too long in the crock "may fall" and bakes "a bit denser",
  so dense has two time-based causes; the log's "barely bubbly" is what separates them, and the
  lesson's objective is exactly this kind of "what would each cause predict". Fix: split, and add
  the over-rise reading with the evidence against it. The comparison of the friend's 7 hours was
  also made against King Arthur's 10 to 12 hours when the friend's recipe is the classic 12 to 18.
- **F5. Quiz 3 re-tested the body's own checkpoint (defect 5b).** The stem (a drier no-knead
  dough, dense and chewy, pockets of flour) was the ATK 70% checkpoint forty lines above, and its
  key was the checkpoint's answer. Replaced with a transfer item: the reader's own 64% kneaded loaf
  made no-knead, predict the crumb.
- **F6. Cold-start: the shopping list and two handling steps were missing.** The kit line named an
  oven, pot, scale and thermometer, but the recipe also needs bread flour, instant yeast (a reader
  may have only active dry), cornmeal or semolina, oven gloves for a lid at 450°F, and a pot whose
  lid knob survives 450°F. "Stir the dough down" was used without saying what it means. Fix: all
  added, with the active dry yeast difference stated as something the course didn't test.
- **F7. Ruhlman trimmed of "with kosher salt", then described as giving no salt.** The quotation
  stops at "a braided loaf"; the source continues "with kosher salt"; the next sentence says the post
  "does not give salt". Accurate about the amount, not about salt. Fix: quote to the end and say the
  post names salt but gives no amount.
- **F8. The lid post's product misattributed.** "King Arthur's own baking cloche". The post sells the
  Brod & Taylor Baking Shell, a third-party product in King Arthur's shop. Fix, and Sources entry
  11 corrected.

**Minor**

- **F9.** "fastest fermentation at 86° to 95°F" dropped the page's "or even higher" and turned
  "optimum" into "fastest". Restored.
- **F10.** "Only water, and liquids that are mostly water ... count towards hydration" hardened
  Philip's "can also be considered". Softened, attributed.
- **F11.** "By King Arthur's chart, ¼ teaspoon is about 0.75 g" presented course arithmetic as the
  chart's row. Reworded to show the division.
- **F12.** "The trade ... a wetter dough gives ... less height of its own" sits against the recipe's
  own "high-rising" blurb and is not something ATK's captions say about this loaf. Reworded to what
  the captions do say and what the pot does.
- **F13.** The CDC's edible-dough sentence dropped its instruction to read the label. Added. The
  FSA's direct statement on "doughs" was not used, so "bread dough ... is covered too" rested on the
  lesson's inference; now quoted, with the CDC's pizza dough.
- **F14.** The final checkpoint called 75% a "limit ATK gives for sandwich breads"; López-Alt
  describes what standard sandwich breads are, not a limit. Reworded, with the 68% test loaf as the
  closer comparison.
- **F15.** "That's why lesson 4's callout ... says" claimed a causal link between lessons; reworded
  to "says the same thing". One paragraph-ending moral ("The salt belongs in the dough.") cut.
- **F16. Media.** No figure or video. The loaf is simple enough that the recipe page's own photos
  serve; not fixed (see below).
