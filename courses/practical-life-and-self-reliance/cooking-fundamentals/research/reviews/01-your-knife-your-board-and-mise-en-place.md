**Findings:**

1. **[high] Quiz 3 explanation, "Glass is easy to clean but dulls a knife fast".** No read source
   says it. SOURCES Part D 3g attributes it to "Cliver's point on knife wear", but neither record of
   the Cliver 2006 abstract mentions knife wear: the provenance is itself unsupported (defect 22).
   Remove the claim; note it in SOURCES. (Drafter flag 6.)
2. **[high] Internal contradiction on cutting direction.** Line 373, "At a home board, most cuts
   are straight down", against the lesson's own line 159, "Most cuts are not a straight chop
   downward", and ATK's "a pushing or pulling action rather than a straight downward cut". Reword to
   say the blade travels down and along the board, not towards you.
3. **[medium] Quiz 3 answerable by shape.** Three distractors each carried an absolute ("nowhere to
   hide", "can never survive", "the one material"); the key was the only option without one. Replace
   the plastic distractor with a non-absolute one built on the lab result it misreads.
4. **[medium] Cold start: no steel, no sharpener.** The equipment list names no honing steel, yet
   the paper test and exercise step 3 say "hone it", and a failed test says "it needs sharpening"
   with nothing on what a reader without a sharpener does. Say the paper test works without a steel,
   give ATK's "run it through a sharpener", and say the course doesn't teach sharpening.
5. **[medium] `minutes:` and the exercise timing (drafter's question).** Measured 95 against the
   outline's ~55. The body is about 41 minutes of reading plus ~12 of think-blocks, quiz and the
   two short videos, which is near the outline's figure; the overrun is the exercise. Its 45 minutes
   is honest for the cutting, measuring, paper test and a mise en place (roughly 10 + 10 + 5 + 15 to
   20), but "Take 45 minutes" also covered "then cook it", which it cannot. Say the 45 excludes the
   cooking, and name the ruler and paper the exercise needs. Do not trim: nothing in the body is
   padding by the standards' test, and cutting and measuring real vegetables is the lesson's core
   practice.
6. **[medium] Unsourced flat claim, "Minced garlic in hot oil can go from golden to burnt in the
   time it takes to find the next ingredient."** Nothing in SOURCES supports it (defect 22, third
   form). Replace with the self-evident point it illustrates. (Drafter flag 4.)
7. **[low] ATK's dull-knife passage quoted without its next sentence**, "The result most often is
   food that's crushed or bruised." The lesson uses the quotation for injury; ATK's own emphasis is
   on the food. Not a reversal, but defect 9's shape. Add the sentence as paraphrase.
8. **[low] McGorry's "cutting moments" rendered as "cutting force"**, in the body and in quiz 5's
   explanation. A moment is a turning effort, not a force. Reword.
9. **[low] Video caption points to "step 6" in a video nobody watched.** The numbering is the
   lesson's, not the video's. Reword and say the video was checked for metadata only. (Drafter flag
   5; metadata confirmed: 103 s, 184 s, 238 s, channels ATK and KQED Food.)
10. **[low] Go deeper calls the Kentucky handout "a one-page summary".** The PDF is eight pages.
11. **[low] "Here's a whole onion taken to a medium dice"** opens a section (defect 7).
12. **[low] "predicts exactly what the pan of badly cut carrots showed"** overstates a labelled
    inference whose own checkpoint calls it a rough guide. Drop "exactly". (Drafter flag 1.)
13. **[low] Mise en place step 1, "and note the tools you'll need"**, cited to Escoffier, whose
    step 1 is reading ingredients, technique and method; gathering tools is its step 2. Drop the
    clause.
14. **[low] Quiz 2 explanation, "pressing harder only risks damaging the edge".** ATK says honing

15. **[low] Cold start: "sheet pan"** is American; gloss it.
16. **[low] "ATK calls that name a misnomer"** is cited only to [10]; the word "misnomer" is in [1].
