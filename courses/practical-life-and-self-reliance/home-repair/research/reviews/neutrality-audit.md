# Neutrality audit: Home Repair and Maintenance, course-wide

## 2026-09-26 — Course-wide neutrality audit (standards 3.5)

**Scope.** All 23 lessons, read in full (body, blocks, quiz items and explanations, Go deeper and
Sources), and the project, `assessments/project-your-homes-maintenance-plan.md`. The final test was
under review by another agent and was left alone. The podcast scripts were read only to check whether
they repeat a sentence this audit changes. The course's commitments were taken from OUTLINE's
eighteen decisions and "Neutrality notes", the conventions in `pipeline-briefs.md`, and the notes in
`reviews/course-wide.md`.

**Method.** Four readers each took a block of lessons (1 to 6, 7 to 12, 13 to 18, and 19 to 23 with
the project). Each kept a ledger with line numbers of every country framing, every endorsement or
doubt in the course's voice, blame, renter mentions, source descriptors, every "Call someone" with
any cheaper route, and the running order and last word of every contested block. The auditor compared
the ledgers across lessons, re-read every passage cited below, and ran course-wide greps for source
descriptors, UK and US lead order, money and landlord mentions. Lessons 8, 18 and 22 each passed their
own neutrality pass. This audit looks for patterns that no single lesson shows.

**Perspectives checked.** A UK reader and a US reader; a renter and an owner; a tradesperson and a
DIY householder; a trade association and an occupational-licensing critic; a remedial damp-proofing
firm and a conservation surveyor; a landlord; a reader on a low income.

**Verdict: PASS WITH FIXES APPLIED.** No contested question is settled in the course's voice. The
three contested blocks (lessons 8, 18, 22) give each side in its own words, and none shows a
consistent last word for one side. No sentence blames tenants or landlords. Twelve single-edit fixes
are applied below. Three patterns stay open because the fixes are larger than one edit: money and
free routes, renters' routes, and the UK as the default reader. The fix for each is written out
under "Open, recommended". None of them settles a contested question, so none blocks publication.
The lesson 8 checkpoint (N1) is the one to do first, because it is where a reader acts on the
scald-against-legionella trade-off.

## What holds across the course

- **Source descriptors are consistent where it matters.** ESF is "a UK charity that campaigns on
  electrical safety" (lessons 6, 13, 14, 22) or "a charity that campaigns on electrical safety"
  (lesson 5). The PCA is "the trade body for damp-proofing firms" (lesson 17) or described in its own
  words (lesson 18). TrustMark "describes itself as" government endorsed (lesson 23). Gas Safe's "both
  parties" line is always the Register's (lesson 10). WRc's interest is named (lesson 4). The course's
  report in lesson 10 that it "couldn't find the provision the Register has in mind" is OUTLINE's
  gate G-G1 finding stated as the course's own search. It does not criticise the Register, and it
  stays.
- **The law-and-advice pair (decision 8) holds everywhere it applies:** gas (10), asbestos and lead
  (19), ladders (15, 16), DIY electrics (5, 22).
- **Blame order (decision 15) holds.** In lessons 17 and 18, GOV.UK's no-blame statement comes before
  every behavioural tip. Lesson 18 names the building as the problem where the home "does not enable"
  moisture control. No sentence in the course's voice blames a landlord either.
- **Lesson 22's licensing block** quotes every advocate, names each side's stake in the same
  paragraph, reports the federal report's endorsement of targeted licensing beside its findings, has
  one misconception from each side, and ends on "The course stops there." The scope decision on
  wiring is stated as a teaching judgement, not a side (lessons 5 and 22).
- **Lesson 18's rising damp block** gives the PCA and the RICS-reported thesis, and the 2009 sceptics,
  each in their own words. It dates and caveats both (sceptics from 2009 only; thesis via summary
  only), puts the 2022 joint statement as common ground, and reaches no verdict.
- **Lesson 8** calls both hazards established and picks no number. Its "Holding both" section ends on
  HSE's care-settings page, the only source read that weighs the two together.
- **The project** gives a renter a route in every part, and a part skipped because the reader rents
  counts as complete.

## Open, recommended (larger than one edit)

In priority order. For each: where, what a reader would object to, and the fix.

### N1. Lesson 8's exercise checkpoint gives the scald side the last word where the reader acts (lines 375 to 382)

*Reader: a UK owner with a stored cylinder; anyone reading HSE.* The body is balanced, but the
checkpoint is where the reader decides, and it tilts. Above 60°C, only the scald side and "lower it"
are given. At or below 49°C, the reading is "CPSC's recommended level", and HSE's concern is narrowed
to a tank "unused for long spells". But HSE's 60°C storage figure applies to any stored hot water,
and 49°C is below HSE's own 50°C outlet example. **Fix:** replace the ≤49°C sentence with:
"**Reading at or below 49°C (120°F):** CPSC's recommended level for scalds. If it comes from a stored
cylinder or tank, it is also below the 50°C outlet figure in HSE's own low-risk example, and HSE's
control for stored water is 60°C; weigh who lives there, as above, and the heater's manual." Then
add "or on legionella, HSE's page" after "Weigh who lives there" in the ≥60°C line. The script does
not carry these lines.
Related, minor: line 245 puts people only on the scald side ("a toddler or a very old person in the
bath") and plumbing on the other ("a store of hot water that sits unused for weeks"). The script
repeats this at 08:61. Line 262 gives HSE's "much lower" at basins but drops the next clause, that
people assessed as vulnerable at basins "should" have controls (in SOURCES). Go deeper 414 calls
HSE's page "the plainest official account read of why domestic legionella risk is usually low";
describe what it covers instead.

### N2. Money: "Call someone" never counts the cost, and the free routes the sources give are scattered (course-wide)

*Reader: someone on a low income; a renter.* Across the 23 lessons there are about 110 "Call someone"
labels. No lesson says what calling someone costs, and the only direct acknowledgement is lesson 21's
line that professional pest control "is often prohibitively expensive" (the lesson 21 script drops
it). Lesson 23 opens by saying every such job goes to "a person you pay". Free or cheaper routes the
sources do give appear once each, away from the "Call someone" lines that need them:
- the free 24-hour gas emergency number (L9);
- HSE's free Gas Safety Advice Line, 0800 300 363 (L10);
- the landlord's annual gas safety check, electrical report and alarm duties (L6, L10, L11);
- the fire and rescue service for alarms (L12);
- the council's environmental health officer (L19, L23);
- a state radon programme for a kit (L19);
- Citizens Advice and Trading Standards (L23);
- the water company where the fault is its side (L2);
- a retailer or maker for a faulty appliance (L6).

Items the course assumes the reader will buy or pay for (plug-in RCD, stand-off, respirator,
hygrometer, detector, a ten-yearly survey, a "second opinion") go by with no mention of cost.

**Fix, one place:** in lesson 23, directly after the Call someone table (line 180), add a short
paragraph headed "Before you pay anyone". It gathers, each with its lesson and source:
- if you rent, the landlord first, for the gas check, electrics, alarms and damp;
- in a gas emergency, the free emergency number;
- for gas advice, HSE's free line;
- for asbestos advice, the council's environmental health officer;
- in the US, a radon kit through the state programme;
- for a faulty appliance, the maker or retailer;
- for a trader dispute, Citizens Advice or Trading Standards (UK), or the FTC or state consumer
  office (US).

Add a matching line to the project's Part 5 (the call list): "and the free route, if there is one".
Don't add prices (decision 10).

### N3. Renters: body methods labelled "Yours" drop the renter condition that exercises carry (lessons 2, 3, 4, 7, 12, 18, 23)

*Reader: a renter; a landlord.* Every exercise carries "if it's your home to repair, or your landlord
agrees". But the same jobs in the lesson body are labelled plain **Yours**, with no renter note:
thawing a pipe (L2 ~362), a washer tap (L3 ~158), a flapper (L3 ~240), removing a sink trap (L4
~465), bleeding a radiator, which can mean a top-up (L7 331, 556). Lesson 1's quiz 1 sends a renter
to an electrician with no landlord. Lesson 23's stop table has no landlord route in any row, gas
included, where the course read the landlord's reg 36 check. Lessons 7 (565) and 11 (221, 580) tell
the reader to book an annual service, with no pointer that in a let home it is the landlord's reg 36
check.

Lesson 12 states England's landlord duty to fit an alarm on each storey (line 128). It leaves out the
same Regulations' duty to check alarms on the first day of a tenancy (reg 4(1)(b)), which lesson 11
states for CO alarms and SOURCES D23 holds.

Lesson 18 contradicts itself in two places:
- Vents are "**Yours**, in a rented home too" (283) but need "your landlord agrees" (672).
- A small mould patch needs the landlord's agreement (329, 675) but is "yours on GOV.UK's account"
  (617). GOV.UK expects tenants to clean very small amounts, so the landlord condition is stricter
  than the source. The script repeats "one where the landlord agrees" at 18:57.

**Fix:** add "(if you rent, the landlord's)" or "if it's your home to repair" at each body **Yours**
listed. Add the reg 36 pointer beside each annual-service line. Add reg 4(1)(b) to lesson 12 at
line 128. In lesson 18, make cleaning a very small patch "yours, and reported if you rent" in all
three places and in the script, and give clearing an existing vent one label in both places.

### N4. The UK is the default reader (course-wide)

*Reader: a US reader.* Where a section has both countries, the UK leads in 10 of the 12 lessons that
carry both (US first only in lessons 11 and 21). Quiz items in lessons 7 to 12 are 21 UK, 9 US and 1
both. Lesson 15 has no US quiz item. UK-only words appear in instructions with no US equivalent at
first use: tip, bin, nappies, washing-up liquid, torch, spanner, flex, A&E, hoover, skirting, loft,
plasterboard, tin, petrol (L11, which the script repeats). Decision 3 asks for names and units both at
first use. Lesson 4's trap seals are in millimetres only. Lesson 7 gives °C with no °F.

In three places a US reader's operative guidance is British, because no US source was read:
- L15's Gulf Coast example ("on HSE's guidance, the ladder does not go on the gutter");
- L17 quiz 5 (Ozarks, roof work on HSE);
- L18 quiz 3 (Galloway, a Scottish reader given England's GOV.UK guidance, labelled "for England" in
  the explanation).

Each is labelled, but none says "no US source on this was read", which lesson 5's UK-only "Is it
you, or the network?" (328 to 340) also lacks.

None of this calls either country wrong. The pattern is order and vocabulary, not voice. **Fix
(next pass of each lesson):** alternate which country leads where a section gives both. Add the US
word at first use for the terms listed. At each of the four places named, add one sentence saying no
US source was read and that the British guidance is the one this course has. The institute's UK
spelling ("mould") stays, per decision 3.

### N5. Praise and grading of sources in the course's voice (course-wide, mostly Go deeper)

*Reader: a trade association or a rival source.* About twenty entries grade a source rather than
describe it. They include "clearest", "plainest", "most practical", "better than any safety leaflet"
(L11 631), "is blunt" or "puts it bluntly", and Dulux "promotes its products, but the clearest
account" (L20). Two touch contested questions:
- L4 628 calls WRc's page "The strongest case read for flushability testing". Only WRc's side of the
  wipes dispute has a Go deeper entry.
- L1 362 to 365, and its quiz, call ESF's flood advice "more careful" than the Met Office's; the
  script repeats this at 01:75. It is a safety nuance, not a contested question, so it was left, but
  "adds a condition" says the same thing without ranking.

**Fix:** where a Go deeper entry grades a source, replace the grade with what the page covers and
for whom. In L4, add The Water Report's page to Go deeper beside WRc's, and change WRc's line to "WRc's
case for flushability testing, from the body that ran the tests". In L11 631, drop "better than any
safety leaflet".

### N6. Lesson 22: two uneven checks, one term not self-used

*Reader: a trade association; a licensing critic.*
- **Uneven caveats.** SELECT's harm figures get a caveat in the course's voice (469 to 470: "As quoted,
  neither figure says how much of the harm came from unqualified work"). The Institute for Justice's
  "one of the most consistent findings" and its fee figures (510 to 516) get none. Fix: after the
  Institute's "higher costs, limited access to services or both.", add "As quoted, it gives no figure
  for how much prices rise in any one trade." This costs about 17 words against the lesson's roughly
  30 of headroom.
- **Quiz item 6** calls the Institute "a free-market group", a term it does not use for itself
  (standards 3.3). Fix: "a group that campaigns for the right to earn a living" (the Institute's
  framing, quoted in the body).
- **One side of the DIY-electrics value question keeps turning up.** ESF's "Any electrical work should
  be left to competent professionals" is quoted in lessons 5, 14 and 22, and in lesson 14 as agreeing
  with the course. The householder-freedom side is never argued, and lesson 22 says so honestly. This
  is a source gap (G-L4), not a voice problem. Fix, next research pass: read one source that argues
  the householder's side in its own words. Until then, lesson 14 193 to 195 reads "ESF's general
  advice, one side of a question lesson 22 sets out, is ...".

### N7. Damp in practice: the damp trade is always the party being checked (lessons 17, 18)

*Reader: a remedial damp-proofing firm.* The rising damp block is balanced. But every worked case,
checkpoint and quiz item where a professional appears casts the damp or waterproofing firm as the
quote to question:
- L17: the Finger Lakes contractor, and "before anyone sells you a machine" (544);
- L18: the Cynon Valley surveyor, the Islay quiz, the opener's "pay for a treatment aimed at a cause
  the house does not have" (111, and script 18:41), and the stop list at 688.

The joint statement's standards (proportion, declared interest, obvious defects first) bind any
surveyor, "whichever side of the argument the surveyor is on", as line 530 says. But no example holds
a conservation-side recommendation to them.

Inside the block there are two smaller asymmetries:
- Only the sceptics' subsection ends with a rebuttal from the other side (Terry Brown, 485 to 489).
- The pro side's subsection is headed with "The research case" (460).

The next paragraph's two-way caveat largely balances both. **Fix, next pass:** make one of the
Islay quiz or the L17 case a surveyor recommending lime repointing or a long monitoring period, and
hold it to the same three questions. Change "The research case" to "The thesis". Leave Brown's reply
in place (it is part of the one article read) but say it is the article's reply.

The script's "Lifestyle. That word again." (18:79) sets the joint statement against GOV.UK more
sharply than the lesson does. Use the lesson's own gloss ("occupation patterns") there.

### N8. The project's automatic fails are not neutral between countries or incomes (project lines 421, 424)

- **The 6 m automatic fail.** "Any job within 6 m of an overhead line" fails the project outright
  for a US reader, although lesson 23's table labels 6 m "(GB)" and lesson 15 gives OSHA's 10 feet.
  Fix: "within 6 m of an overhead line in Great Britain, or OSHA's 10 feet (about 3 m) in the US
  (lesson 15)".
- **The cash automatic fail.** "Paying a contractor in cash" fails the project outright in both
  countries. That turns Citizens Advice's "Don't pay with cash" (UK advice) into a safety-grade rule,
  and it fails a reader with no card for writing down the payment they can make. Fix: move cash to
  the rubric ("sound" names a traceable payment, as lesson 23 advises). Keep wire transfer, gift
  card and signing over an insurance check as automatic fails for the US, where lesson 23 treats
  them as scam signs.

### N9. Smaller items

- **The EST descriptor.** "The UK's government-funded energy advice body" (L11 223, L18 267) comes from SOURCES line 769, not
  from the Trust's own description of itself. Check it against the EST's About page at the next
  SOURCES pass. This audit did not edit SOURCES.
- **Jeff Howell's descriptor.** He is "a qualified bricklayer and the author" (L18); the other side
  has "chartered surveyors" and "a doctoral thesis". If the AJ article gives more, use it; if not,
  the asymmetry is the source's.
- **The Ladder Association** co-authors LA455 and is never described as a trade association (L15,
  L16). Neither are Citizens Advice, WaterSafe, HETAS or CSIA. Describe each once at first use.
- **An unsourced line in lesson 23.** Lines 137 to 139 say the doorstep trader "may not be
  qualified, registered, insured or honest", with no source. Attribute it to Personal Safety lesson 5
  or cut "or honest".
- **Lesson 21: glue traps and woodworm.**
  - Every quoted voice on glue traps is against their use on rodents. That is the law's direction in
    all three GB nations, so it is stated as law, not opinion. Keep it that way.
  - The woodworm section is the council's conservation view, and the treatment firms' view is named
    as "search snippets" only. That matches OUTLINE's note ("not characterised from snippets"), but
    extending the council's surveyor advice to every house is the course's reading. Label it so at
    that sentence.
