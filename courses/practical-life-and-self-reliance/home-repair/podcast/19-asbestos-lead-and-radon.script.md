S1: For Great Britain, HSE says it may be part of any building built or refurbished before the year two thousand. Refurbished matters, because a part of the house can be much younger than the house. HSE's list includes asbestos cement pipes, flues and roofs, lagging on pipes and boilers, water tanks, insulating board, which closely resembles typical plasterboard, and textured decorative coatings, commonly called Artex. The US Environmental Protection Agency gives no year. It says you generally can't tell by looking, and if in doubt, treat the material as if it contains asbestos and leave it alone.

S2: So if I think I've got some?

S1: HSE says it's often best to leave it where it is, especially if it's in good condition and unlikely to be damaged, and to check its condition from time to time. EPA says leave undamaged material alone. Looking at it now and then, without touching it, is Yours.

S2: Now the law. Could I legally take it out myself?

S1: In Great Britain, the Control of Asbestos Regulations twenty twelve put their duties on employers, and apply them to the self employed. The Health and Safety at Work Act defines work as work as an employee or as a self employed person, and self employed means working for gain or reward. No HSE page the course read says in so many words whether the Regulations reach a householder doing a job in their own home. The course's reading of that text is that they don't. They bind the builder or electrician in your house, not an owner occupier doing their own job. That's the course's reading, not a ruling, and not something HSE says.

S2: So legally, I'm in the clear.

S1: Not quite. The asbestos Regulations don't stop you, on the course's reading. But waste law does reach a householder, at least in England and Wales, the only part of the UK whose waste law the course read. HSE says asbestos has to be disposed of as hazardous waste, not mixed with household waste, and handled only by a licensed disposal site. And in England and Wales, the Environmental Protection Act nineteen ninety puts a duty on the occupier of a home to take reasonable measures so that household waste they hand on goes only to an authorised person.
