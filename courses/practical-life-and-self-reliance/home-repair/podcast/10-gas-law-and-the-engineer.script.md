S2: Welcome in. This is the Foval Learning Institute podcast, where the two of us talk a lesson through. I'm Haley.

S1: And I'm John. Today we're in Home Repair and Maintenance, on the lesson called Gas: the law and the engineer.

S2: Mostly law, so the usual lines first.

S1: This course is education, not a trade qualification, and it teaches no gas work and no wiring. And for the law, the lesson's own label. This is general information, not legal advice. The rules differ between England, Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland, and between US states and cities, and they change.

S2: Lesson one gave the gas law in two lines.

S1: Registration binds those who do gas work as a business. Competence binds everyone, householders included. In England, Wales and Scotland that's regulation three of the Gas Safety, Installation and Use, Regulations of nineteen ninety eight. Its first paragraph: no person shall carry out any work in relation to a gas fitting or gas storage vessel unless he is competent to do so. No person means anyone.

S2: And the Register?

S1: That's the third paragraph. An employer can't let staff do gas work, and a self-employed person can't do it, unless they belong to the class of people the Health and Safety Executive has approved. HSE's page says a gas business must be on the Gas Safe Register. Notice who it names. Employers and the self-employed. A householder working on their own home is neither.

S2: If a friend fits my hob for nothing, is that even work?

S1: It is. HSE's book on the Regulations, called L fifty six, says in paragraph fifty two that work includes do it yourself, favours for friends and relatives, and work with no expectation of reward, so anyone doing it must have the necessary competence. But membership of the approved class is required only by businesses carrying out gas fitting work. Payment matters to registration, not to competence.

S2: Then what counts as competent?

S1: L fifty six carries HSE's Approved Code of Practice, which has a special legal status. Its paragraph eighty one says gas work should only be undertaken, first, by a person who has successfully completed an industry recognised training course followed by assessment of competence. Its other routes are for people who are or were registered and have proved competence through a certification scheme, and for premises outside the Regulations. And paragraph eighty three says do it yourself gas engineers, and people doing favours for friends and relatives, all need the required competence.

S2: So is it illegal to do gas work yourself, or not?

S1: Under the gas safety regulations, "it's illegal to do any gas work yourself" overstates the law, because the registration duty doesn't reach a householder. And "it's legal, so it's fine" misleads, because the competence duty does, measured by a recognised course and an assessment. And HSE strongly advises that all gas appliances, flues and pipework be installed, regularly maintained and serviced at least annually by a Gas Safe registered engineer, and the Register says don't DIY with gas appliances, always use a Gas Safe registered engineer. So for a householder in Great Britain, gas work is call someone.

S2: The lesson has a case.

S1: Still general information, not legal advice. Sam, in Rutland, in England, has bought a new gas hob. His brother in law is handy, has fitted kitchens, isn't registered, and offers to put it in on Saturday for nothing. Is it gas work? Yes. Does registration reach him? An unpaid favour isn't a business, so on those words, no. Does competence? Yes. No person, and paragraph eighty three names favours. Fitting kitchens isn't a recognised course and an assessment.

S2: He says a registered engineer could check it afterwards.

S1: The Gas Safe Register says: it is illegal for someone who is not Gas Safe registered to fit a gas appliance or do other gas work and then have the work checked by a Gas Safe registered engineer. Both parties would be breaking the law. That's the Register's statement. If the first person does gas work as a business, regulation three covers them. But for an unpaid helper who does have the competence, the course read regulation three and HSE's guidance and couldn't find the provision the Register has in mind, so it reports the sentence as Gas Safe's, not as the law. Either way, a check afterwards doesn't supply the competence the law asks of the person doing the work. Sam's hob is call someone.

S2: Change one thing. He asks for sixty pounds to cover his time, and he does jobs like this for other people.

S1: This part is the course's reading, not a ruling on anyone's case. The Regulations were made under the Health and Safety at Work Act, and a general rule of interpretation gives their words that Act's meaning, unless they say otherwise. Section fifty three defines a self-employed person as an individual who works for gain or reward otherwise than under a contract of employment. Sixty pounds for his time is reward. So on the course's reading he'd be doing it as a self-employed person, and the registration duty would reach him too.

S2: So what's left for me?

S1: The Register says you can perform the tasks set out in the user's instructions, provided by the manufacturer, that are intended for the user, and replace or adjust any component designed to be operated or replaced by the consumer, like a cooker's tap control knob. Nothing that disturbs the gas carrying parts or could affect combustion. So user tasks are yours, on conditions, and the condition is that the appliance's own user instructions give you the job.

S2: How do I check the engineer?

S1: The ID card. The front has a photograph, a registration number and an expiry date. The back lists the categories of work they're qualified for, and that's the part people skip. Priya is having her combi boiler replaced. The photograph matches, the card's in date, and boilers are on the back. She enters the number on the Register's Check an Engineer service on its website, and the name, photograph and qualifications match. So she lets him start. If you can't tell which number to enter, HSE gives the Register's freephone helpline as oh eight hundred, four oh eight, five five oh oh.

S2: And in the US?

S1: The course found no single rule. Columbia Gas of Ohio says a qualified professional should do all work on natural gas lines and equipment inside the home. New York City's Department of Buildings says even ordinary repair work that needs no permit, gas piping included, must be done by a Licensed Master Plumber. And Texas's licence law doesn't require a property owner to be licensed for plumbing, gas piping included, in their own homestead. That says nothing about permits, which are local. So ask your city or county building department, and your gas utility, before anyone touches a gas line. On the advice read, it's call someone.
