S1: The same tap can hurt two ways. On US figures, water from a heater set at sixty degrees Celsius, a hundred and forty Fahrenheit, can give most adults a third-degree burn in six seconds. And on British guidance, stored water that isn't kept hot and moving can grow the bacteria behind Legionnaires' disease, a potentially fatal pneumonia. The advice on each is clear, and they pull opposite ways.

S2: Whose advice is whose?

S1: The scald figures are American, from the US Consumer Product Safety Commission. The legionella guidance is British, from the Health and Safety Executive, and it's written for landlords in Great Britain. The course read no UK source on scald temperatures in private homes, and no US source on legionella in homes. So it can't tell you how either country's authorities weigh the other hazard in a home. And the Commission's scald sheet says nothing about legionella, while HSE's legionella page says nothing about scalds.

S2: The scald side, in its own words.

S1: The Commission says most tap water scald injuries and deaths are to the elderly and children under the age of five. Its times: most adults will suffer third-degree burns at a hundred and fifty Fahrenheit, about sixty-six Celsius, in two seconds. Six seconds at a hundred and forty. Thirty seconds at a hundred and thirty. And even at a hundred and twenty, about forty-nine Celsius, a five-minute exposure could result in third-degree burns. It urges all users to lower their water heaters to a hundred and twenty Fahrenheit. And it says always hand-test before using, especially when bathing infants and young children.

S2: And the legionella side.

S1: HSE says all man-made hot and cold water systems are likely to provide an environment where legionella can grow. One control it names is setting the cylinder so water is stored at sixty degrees. The same page's example of a typical low-risk home has instantaneous or low-volume heaters supplying outlets at fifty degrees. And it says the risk in most homes is generally considered low, owing to regular water use, and lower again where there's no storage, with a combi boiler or an electric shower.

That's the course's reading of HSE's page, which gives no figure for how much the risk changes. Both hazards are established. Which way a household leans is a judgement about who lives there. S1: The Commission's check is a candy or meat thermometer. Hold it under the hot tap first thing in the morning, or at least two hours after water was last used. That test is yours.
