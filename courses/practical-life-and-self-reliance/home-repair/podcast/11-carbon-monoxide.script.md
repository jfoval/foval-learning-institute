S2: So where does the gas come from?

S1: The Health and Safety Executive calls carbon monoxide a colourless, odourless, tasteless, poisonous gas. It's produced by incomplete burning of carbon based fuels, including gas, oil, wood and coal. HSE says the fuels are safe to use. It's only when the fuel doesn't burn properly that excess carbon monoxide is produced. So anything that burns fuel is a possible source, including anything with an engine.

S2: Where do the rules put an alarm?

S1: This is general information, not legal advice. The rules differ between England, Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland, and between US states and cities, and they change. In England, a landlord of a tenancy the twenty fifteen Regulations cover must make sure there's a carbon monoxide alarm in any room used wholly or partly as living accommodation that has a fixed combustion appliance other than a gas cooker. For a new or replacement appliance, the law is requirement J three of the Building Regulations twenty ten. In the statute's own words: where a combustion appliance is provided, appropriate provision having regard to the design and location of the appliance shall be made to detect and give early warning of the release of carbon monoxide at levels harmful to persons. It applies only to fixed combustion appliances in dwellings.

The course read no English rule that asks an owner occupier to add an alarm to an appliance already in place.
