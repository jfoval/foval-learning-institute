# Course-wide neutrality audit: Personal Safety

**Date:** 2026-09-25
**Scope:** lessons 1 to 16 and `assessments/project-your-household-safety-plan.md`. The final test
was left alone, because another agent was completing it.
**Against:** standards Part 3; OUTLINE decisions 7, 8, 9 and 15 and each lesson's neutrality note;
the Course conventions in `pipeline-briefs.md`; `reviews/course-wide.md`.

## Verdict: PASS WITH FIXES APPLIED

Seven single-sentence fixes were applied (F1 to F7). Four of them corrected the pattern this audit
exists to catch: the course's own voice taking a side, or overclaiming, in a block it says it leaves
open.

Fourteen findings remain for a fixer (N1 to N14), each with a location and an exact fix. None of them
blocks publication:
- no claim is misclassified;
- every advocacy position quoted passes the ideological Turing test in its own words;
- nothing puts blame on a victim, or on a bystander who froze or who stepped in.

The largest open item is a pattern, not a sentence (N4): almost every threatening person in the
course is a man, and no woman appears as a partner abuser or as a sexual offender.

## How it was read

- **All 16 lessons and the project:** each was read in full by a fresh-context reader. Each reader
  extracted:
  - every person in every scenario, quiz stem and exercise, with sex and role;
  - every two-sided block, with the order of sides, rough word counts and who gets the last word;
  - every course-voice position;
  - every possible blame reading;
  - every repeated statistic and how it is framed;
  - every mention of police, of the US against the UK, and of guns.

  The auditor then checked each candidate against the text, and read the key blocks in full
  directly: lessons 6, 7, 9, 10, 12, 13, 15 and 16.
- **Lesson 1:** also read in full by the auditor.
- **Searched across all 16 lessons and the project:** "should have", "mistake", "could have", "just
  leave", "ought to", loaded gun terms ("gun lobby", "gun nut", "grabber", "loophole",
  "common-sense"), "shoot-first", "so-called", and gendered pronouns on offenders.

**Resistance training.** Before the fixes, this question had the same shape in two lessons. In
lessons 6 and 12 the course said it took no side, then closed the block with the critics' point in

**Guns.** The gun blocks have a milder version. In lessons 13 and 15 the gun-control side, or
evidence read against gun rights, closes each position block. Lesson 15's survivor pair runs the
other way. Recorded as N1.

**Discounts applied to one side.** Three sentences discounted one side only:
- the US alarm studies, for being from "a different country" (lesson 5, F6);

  result from the same paper was not discounted (lesson 15, F4);

  labelled as the course's observation and is kept. It is balanced by the table naming each

### 2. Which sex is the victim, and which the threat

The women who threaten are few:
- two stalkers in lesson 7: the gift-sender in quiz 2, and Rhys's ex-partner;
- a queue-jumper shouting at a father in lesson 9;
- one hypothetical partner in lesson 6.

What is missing:
- No woman appears as a violent or coercive partner.
- No woman appears as a sexual offender. Lesson 11's answer key writes "A man raped a woman" where
  the exercise's own sentence named no perpetrator.
- In lesson 7, every coercive-control case is a man abusing a woman.
- In lesson 12, men appear in the sexual-violence material only as perpetrators or as the target of
  prevention. Male victims of sexual assault are not mentioned, although lesson 11 says "lesson 12
  says so again".

How to read the pattern:
- Victims are split sensibly by crime, and decision 7 (men as victims) is met.
- The sources explain much of the threatener skew: ONS's 98.0% male perpetrators for rape, and
  Refuge's "most ... carried out by men".
- But a pattern this uniform, 90 or more to 6, goes beyond what the figures require. The course
  gives no figure for the sex of offenders in stranger violence, robbery or partner abuse.
- A male survivor of partner abuse finds no case like his.

This is N4.

### 3. Blame

No sentence found implies that anyone should have done otherwise:
- not a victim of any crime;
- not a bystander who froze;
- not someone who stepped in, or who used force.

Every "should have" is either a refusal of blame (L5:134, L6:130, L10:505, L11:483, L12:149,
L13:671, L14:417, L14:573, L16:139, project:124) or quoted law (Scotland's jury direction).

Every lesson that raises a choice under threat closes it with a responsibility line. The weakest
spots, which are mitigated but worth a fixer's eye, are:
- lesson 9's shop robbery, where the stepper-in "puts themselves inside a situation" and the
  no-blame line in that section covers only the person who froze (N12);
- lesson 13's drunk-woman checkpoint (N6).

### 4. The course's own voice on contested questions

After the fixes, the course's voice labels its own readings ("on this course's reading") and closes

## Findings for a fixer

**N1. Gun position blocks: the order (lessons 13 and 15). Minor.**
- **The issue.** Both lessons quote the gun-rights side first and the gun-control side second.
  Lesson 13 then goes to RAND's "supportive" grade on homicides. Lesson 13's "What stand your ground
  doesn't change" ends on "part of what Giffords objects to". Lesson 15's general pair ends on
  Giffords' "Access to a gun triples a person's risk of suicide", an empirical claim from research
  the course did not read.
- **Fix.**
  - In lesson 15, in the paragraph beginning "At the general level" (around line 573), put the
    Giffords sentence first and the NRA-ILA sentence second.
  - In lesson 13, end the Florida-immunity paragraph (around line 617) on the statute's text and
    move "and it is part of what Giffords objects to" to the paragraph's first sentence.

**N2. Household gun risk: the critique is unread (lesson 15). Known; Gate G17.**
- **The issue.** The risk side has a quantified meta-analysis. The critics' methods case is unread,
  and the lesson says so ("that gap is the course's, not theirs"). Two of the three items it offers
  for the other side, the injury rates and "24% fired", are about defensive use, not household risk.
- **Fix.** Close G17 (read a published methods critique of the household studies, as SOURCES names
  it) and give its argument in one paragraph beside Anglemyer's limits.
- **Until then.** Add "on defensive use rather than household risk" after "What it can give from
  what it read" (around line 521), so the substitution is visible.

**N3. The police question: strongest forms (lesson 9, around line 475). Minor.**
- **The issue.** Only the case for calling is given "at its strongest, as this course reads it".
- **Fix.** After "That is an organisation's stated position...", add:

  > "Put at its strongest, as this course reads it, the case for asking first runs like this. The
  > person harassed has already had one choice taken from them, and a call made over their head
  > takes another. A police response brings its own risks, which fall on them, not on the caller,
  > and Right To Be names communities for whom those risks are higher. And they are the one who will
  > live with what follows."

  Every clause comes from Right To Be's stated reasons.

**N4. Men as the threat, almost without exception (course-wide). Moderate; the main open item.**
- **The issue.** See pattern 2 above.
- **Exact fixes.** Each is small, and together they break the uniformity without inventing any
  figure.
  - (a) **Lesson 11**, practice answer key (around line 623): change "A man raped a woman who was
    walking home from a party." to "Someone raped a woman who was walking home from a party." The
    exercise's own sentence named no perpetrator.
  - (b) **Lesson 7**, exercise (around line 700): make Euan's colleague a man whose partner, a
    woman, controls his money, and change the pronouns in the answer (c) to match. This gives the
    lesson one coercive-control case with a male victim.
  - (c) **Lesson 12**, "The gap, said out loud" (around line 500): after "no controlled evidence on
    self-defence classes for men", add "including men who have been sexually assaulted, whom lesson
    11 names", to keep lesson 11's promise.
  - (d) **Lesson 13**: see N6.
  - (e) For the course test and the podcast scripts: vary the sex of the threatening person where a
    stem needs one.
  - (f) For the OUTLINE (decision 7): add a line that threatening people are not always men, beside
    "men are victims in this course too".

**N5. "Mainstream ... not a fringe one" (lesson 1 around line 473; lesson 6 around lines 471 to 473;

  - In lesson 1, delete "That critique is a mainstream position in UK research on violence against

**N6. The drunk woman (lesson 13, checkpoint around line 270). Moderate; survivor reading.**

**N7. Stand your ground: the pro side's physical stake (lesson 13, around lines 514 to 516).

**N8. "The fairest single statement" (lesson 15, around line 372, and the misconception around line

- **Fix.**

  - In the misconception, change the last sentence to "The National Academies report calls it

**N9. The householder rule gets only reasons for it (lesson 14, around lines 170 to 213). Minor;

- **Lesson 14, the issue.** The Government's reason and the High Court's ruling are followed by the

  which reads as a third reason, straight after "without taking a side".
- **Lesson 14, fix.** Change it to "and you have what this course reads as the rule's own logic:
  more room in the home because the situation gives less room to think. That is an account of the
  rule, not an argument for it."
- **Lesson 6, the issue.** "The argument about advice aimed at women" never notes that street advice
  is seldom aimed at men, whom lesson 6's own figure puts most at risk from strangers.
- **Lesson 6, fix.** Add one sentence at the end of "Where the street sits": "The same figures raise
  the opposite question too, which the argument below doesn't take up: advice about the street is
  seldom aimed at the young men who, on these surveys, meet most of its violence."

**N10. Omission for a partner-abuse survivor (lesson 14). Moderate.**

**N11. "Carry pepper spray when you travel" under "What people get wrong" (lesson 6, around line

**N12. The bystander who stepped into a robbery (lesson 9, around lines 427 to 453). Minor.**

**N13. Lessons 1 to 4, smaller course-voice items. Minor.**
