S2: Now the part I think most people get wrong. If I can't speak?

S1: In the UK, on a mobile, the Independent Office for Police Conduct's Silent Solution page explains it. A recorded message tells callers who can't talk to press five five to be put through to the police. Staying silent and not pressing five five means the call is ended. So a silent call alone brings nobody. And its guide says pressing five five does not let the police track your location. The guide doesn't say whether it works the same way in Scotland and Northern Ireland.

S2: So, on a mobile, press five five when you're prompted.

S1: That's it. And it's always best to speak to the operator if you can, even in a whisper. If you can't speak, answer the operator's questions by coughing or tapping the handset, if you can. The cough or tap answers questions; the five five is what gets the call put through. From a landline the five five step isn't used: a call where the operator hears only background noise is put through to the police. In the US, you can text nine one one where that's available, but call if you can.

S2: Okay. Say it's over, nobody's in danger. Do I report?

S1: That's your decision. What the lesson gives you is what each choice leads to, and first, support doesn't depend on a report. In England and Wales, the government's code for victims says access to support is free, even if the crime hasn't been reported. Victim Support, Victim Support Scotland and Victim Support in Northern Ireland all offer help whether or not you've reported, and the Scottish service will talk through whether to. In the US, VictimConnect is a hotline by call, text or chat, with one condition, in its own words: we can only talk, text, or chat with you if you are currently physically safe.

S2: So what does reporting add?

S1: In England and Wales, a crime reference number, and a referral to a support service within two working days. And the clearest case: criminal injuries compensation, for England, Wales and Scotland. The crime has to be a violent one, and it must be reported to the police before you apply. You usually have to claim within two years. In the US, every state runs its own programme, and the course read no state's rules, so it can't tell you whether yours needs a report. What a report doesn't get you matters too. No page the course read promises an arrest, a charge or your property back.
