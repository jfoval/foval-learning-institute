S1: Not yet. The AA, a motoring organisation that sells breakdown cover and repairs, puts the first rule in one line: never try to check anything under the bonnet with the engine running. Ford's handbook for the twenty twenty four Maverick, sold in the US and Canada, prints its own instruction twice, once for the hybrid's high-voltage battery and once for the petrol truck's Auto-Start-Stop. Apply the parking brake, shift into park, switch the ignition off and remove the key before you open the hood or have any service or repair work completed. And unless the ignition is off, Ford says a hybrid's engine could restart at any time. A silent hybrid showing READY is not off.

S2: An electric car has no engine to restart, though.

S1: But it has a fan. Nissan's handbook for the twenty twenty four LEAF, sold in the US, says its automatic cooling fan may come on at any time without warning, even if the power switch is not in the ACC, ON or READY to drive position. So a car can be switched off and still move a part under the bonnet. Nissan's next step, for anyone working near that fan, is to disconnect the twelve volt battery's negative cable first. This course doesn't teach that, as its own scope decision, so it keeps your hands away from the fan. Coolant and brake fluid are read by looking. And if the dipstick or a filler sits next to the fan on your car, and your handbook says to disconnect the battery before working there, that job is call someone.

S1: Deciding whether to add any, because the sources give different rules. Ford, for the Maverick: if the oil level is between the maximum and minimum marks, the oil level is acceptable. Do not add oil. The AA, for any car: if the top of the oil streak is between the minimum and maximum marks, the level is fine. If the level is below halfway between the marks, then we would recommend adding some oil.

S2: So a stick a little under halfway gets two answers.

S1: It does. Ford's is the maker's instruction for its own engine. The AA's is a motoring organisation's general advice. For a Maverick, Ford's governs. For your car, your handbook governs.

S2: How much goes in?

S1: The AA says the difference between the minimum and maximum notches on a dipstick is about a litre of oil. And it says putting too much oil into your car is just as bad as having too little. Ford says oil levels above the maximum mark may cause engine damage. So a top-up goes in a little at a time, with a wait and a re-check between.

S2: So what's mine?

S1: Reading the dipstick is yours. Adding oil is yours, on conditions: the handbook's grade, level ground, an engine that's off and not hot, a little at a time, and never past the maximum. Oil that's milky, which the AA says means coolant or water is leaking into it, or a level you're topping up more than usual, is call someone.

S2: Coolant. Why all the warnings about the cap?

S1: Nissan says the reservoir has a pressure type cap, and for the LEAF, never remove it when the motor compartment is hot. Serious burns could be caused by high-pressure fluid escaping from the radiator. Ford says coolant expands when it's hot. Put those together, and this is this course's reading, not any one source's sentence: hot, the coolant has expanded, the system holds it under pressure behind that cap, and take the cap off and the pressure takes hot fluid with it.

S2: How long do I wait?

S1: Ford tells Maverick owners to wait ten minutes, then put a thick cloth over the cap and open it slowly. Nissan says wait until the motor compartment cools down. Your own handbook's wait is the one to follow. Where it gives none, this course's rule is stricter than either: wait until it's cold.

S2: And reading it?

S1: Cold, through the side of the reservoir, against MIN and MAX, cap shut. That's yours. Topping it up is yours, on conditions: only cold, only the coolant the handbook specifies, and only to MAX. A level that drops, or one you keep making up, is call someone. Brake fluid is read the same way, cap on, and that's yours too. Anything beyond reading it, adding fluid included, is call someone in this course. On Nissan's handbook for the LEAF, below MIN means don't drive until the brake system has been checked.

S1: General information. In Great Britain, regulation thirty four, paragraph six, of the Road Vehicles Construction and Use Regulations nineteen eighty six says every wiper and washer fitted in accordance with this regulation shall at all times while a vehicle is being used on a road be maintained in efficient working order and be properly adjusted. Nothing in it limits that to rain. And DVSA's MOT manual lists windscreen washers not working or not providing sufficient fluid to clear the windscreen as a Major defect, which fails the test.
