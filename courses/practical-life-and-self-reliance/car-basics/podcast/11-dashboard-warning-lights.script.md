S1: ISO's code is voluntary. It binds where a regulation adopts it, and two regulations adopt different amounts. UN Regulation one twenty one is the international rule used for approving cars in many countries. The course read a two thousand and fourteen copy, so later amendments may be missing. It makes the oil pressure, coolant temperature and charging lights red, and the check-engine and anti-lock lights yellow. The US federal standard, F M V S S one oh one, makes the brake system light red, and the anti-lock, stability control and tyre pressure lights yellow. For oil, coolant temperature and charging, it sets the symbol and leaves the colour to the maker.

S2: So on a US car, federal law doesn't make the oil light red.

S1: Right, the colour is the maker's choice. And one row makes the point. The warning that the brake pads are worn is yellow under the UN regulation and red under the US standard, for cars covered by the US brake standard. Same condition, two colours, both lawful where they apply.

S1: The Highway Code's Annex six, advice for Great Britain, explains it: "When you turn the ignition key, warning lights will be illuminated but will go out when the engine starts (except the handbrake warning light). If they do not, or if they come on while you are driving, stop and investigate the problem, as you could have a serious fault." Its next paragraph: if the charge light comes on while you're driving, it may mean the battery isn't charging, and should also be checked as soon as possible to avoid loss of power to lights and other electrical systems.

For temperature, Ford's twenty twenty four Maverick handbook: "If it illuminates, safely stop your vehicle and switch your vehicle off. Have your vehicle checked as soon as possible."
