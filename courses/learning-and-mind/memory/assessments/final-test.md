  # --- Reconstruction, and what this course is for (L1) ---
  - q: >-
      What does it mean to say a remembered event is reconstructed rather than retrieved?
    options:
      - That the memory decays between one recall and the next, so that less of it comes back
      - That remembering is unreliable, so no account of an old event can be trusted
      - That the account is built at the time of remembering, from what is available then
      - That a memory is stored as a summary and the details are filled in by guessing
    answer: 2
    explain: >-
      An account assembled at the moment of recall is a different thing from a file read off a
      disc, and what is available at that moment includes things that were not there at the time.
      Decay is a separate idea about storage. Unreliability is a verdict rather than a mechanism.
      And "guessing" makes the process sound deliberate, which is not what the account says.
  - q: >-
      A colleague and you remember the same meeting differently, and both accounts are detailed and
      confident. What does this course let you conclude?
    options:
      - That two accounts can differ without either person being careless or dishonest
      - That at least one of you has been led by somebody's question about the meeting
      - That the more detailed account is likelier to be the accurate one of the two
      - That the disagreement will resolve if you both write the accounts down separately
    answer: 0
    explain: >-
      Two accounts built at different moments from different available material is the ordinary
      case, and nothing about it requires anybody to be at fault. A leading question is one
      mechanism and this course cannot say it operated here. Detail is not evidence of accuracy.
      And writing them down records the difference rather than settling it.
  - q: >-
      Which of these did this course deliberately not teach, and say so?
    options:
      - What forgetting looks like when somebody plots it over a month
      - What a leading question does to what people later report seeing
      - What confidence in an identification is worth under good procedures
      - What happens to memory in ageing, and clinical memory disorders
    answer: 3
    explain: >-
      Both are different literatures this course did not search, and it names the omission rather
      than covering them lightly. The other three are its own lessons.

  # --- The forgetting curve and the method of savings (L2) ---
  - q: >-
      What is the method of savings, and what was it measured on in the 2015 replication?
    options:
      - A proportion of items recalled, measured in one subject learning nonsense syllables
      - Time saved on relearning, measured in one subject learning nonsense syllables
      - The number of repetitions to mastery, measured across six intervals in one subject
      - Recognition of material that cannot be produced, measured over a month in one adult
    answer: 1
    explain: >-
      The measure is how much of the original learning effort relearning spares you, and the
      replication ran one subject over seventy hours across six intervals from twenty minutes to
      thirty-one days. A proportion recalled is the thing savings is constantly mistaken for.
      Repetitions to mastery and recognition are two other instruments again.
  - q: >-
      A corporate trainer says "research shows people forget most of what they learn in a day, so
      our refresher modules are essential". What is the first thing wrong with it?
    options:
      - The claim reports one instrument's result as though it came from another
      - Nothing, since that is what the forgetting curve has consistently shown
      - The refresher modules are not what the forgetting research recommends instead
      - The research is from the 1880s, so it cannot describe how anybody learns now
    answer: 0
    explain: >-
      What was measured is savings on relearning and what is being claimed is production, which is
      the swap this course opens with. The curve does not show that claim. Whether refreshers help
      is a separate question this course did not read about. And age is not what makes an
      instrument wrong.
  - q: >-
      Why does this course use a study with one participant at all?
    options:
      - Because a single participant measured exhaustively gives a more precise population estimate
      - Because it is the only study of forgetting that has ever been successfully replicated
      - Because replicating a one-subject study with one subject tests whether the result holds up
      - Because the effect is so large that a bigger sample would add nothing to it
    answer: 2
    explain: >-
      Testing whether an older result reproduces is a real question with a narrow answer, and the
      original was also one subject. One person is not a sample of a population however much data
      you take. Plenty of forgetting research has been replicated. And nothing here reports an
      effect size that would make a sample unnecessary.

  # --- Retrieval-induced forgetting (L3) ---
  - q: >-
      A revision group divides a syllabus and each member tests themselves only on their own
      section. What does this literature predict?
    options:
      - Each member's untested sections will be lost entirely by the end of the fortnight
      - Each member's practised sections will be stronger and neighbouring ones slightly weaker
      - Nothing, since the effect only appears when the same person practises everything
      - The group will do better overall than anybody testing themselves across the whole syllabus
    answer: 1
    explain: >-
      A benefit on what was practised and a cost of a few percentage points on related material
      that was not is the shape of the finding. Nothing here supports erasure. The effect is about
      what one person retrieved and what sat beside it. And whether the division helps the group is
      a question about the exam rather than about memory.
  - q: >-
      A review reports an effect of 9 percent overall, 12 percent in studies with one design
      feature absent and 5 percent in studies with it present. What should a reader take?
    options:
      - That the overall figure is the best estimate, since it uses all the available studies
      - That the lower figure is the true one and the higher studies should be discarded
      - That the phenomenon is too unstable to have been worth pooling in the first place
      - That a single figure here is as much a statement about method as about the phenomenon
    answer: 3
    explain: >-
      A number that halves under one design choice cannot be quoted without saying which kind of
      study produced it, which is what the split is for. Averaging two designs describes neither.
      Studies without a control are not worthless, they measure something else as well. And
      reporting the split is what makes the literature readable.
  - q: >-
      How To Learn Anything recommends retrieval practice as the best technique available, and this
      course reports that retrieval makes neighbouring material harder to reach. How do they fit?
    options:
      - The earlier course is out of date, since this literature is more recent than its sources
      - They contradict each other, and the honest position is that the question is unresolved
      - They are about different groups of items, and the benefit is much larger than the cost
      - They agree, because both find that retrieval strengthens whatever a learner practises
    answer: 2
    explain: >-
      A large benefit on what you practised and a small cost on what sat beside it are two
      measurements of two sets of items, and the practical consequence is to spread your testing
      rather than to stop. Neither course is out of date. The two are not in contradiction. And the
      cost on unpractised neighbours is not something both literatures find.

      The course could reach an examination board's resource pages and not the paper, and says so

  - q: >-
      An HR investigator asks a witness "how aggressive was he when he interrupted you?" What does
      this course support saying about it?
    options:
      - That the wording supplies material the account may take up, which is worth avoiding
      - That the answer will simply be false, since a leading question implants a false memory
      - That the answer is unusable, so the investigation should be started again
      - That the risk is real only if the witness is asked the same question repeatedly
    answer: 0
    explain: >-
      A word that carries a frame is the thing the experiments varied, and the consequence is that
      the account may include what the word brought rather than what happened. Calling the answer
      false goes past evidence that is consistent with two mechanisms, one of which changes no
      memory at all. Nothing here says an investigation must restart. And repetition is a different
      variable that this course did not read about.
  - q: >-
      You trace a widely repeated figure and find the original is behind a paywall you cannot
      reach, while three summaries agree on the number. What have you established?
    options:
      - That the figure is reliable, since three independent sources report the same thing
      - That the figure is unreliable, since nobody in the chain has produced the original
      - That the trace failed, so the figure should not be used or mentioned at all
      - That the figure is unverified, and agreement among summaries is not verification
    answer: 3
    explain: >-
      Summaries copy each other, so agreement is what you would see whether the number is right or
      wrong, and the honest report is the read level rather than a verdict. Nothing about a paywall
      makes a figure wrong. And a figure held at one remove can be used as long as the lesson says
      that is what it is, which is what this course does.

  # --- Confidence and accuracy (L5) ---
  - q: >-
      A police force runs lineups with several suspects in them and takes confidence statements at
      the trial. What does the synthesis say about its identifications?
    options:
      - That high confidence there is still a strong indicator, though a weaker one than usual
      - That its conclusions cannot yet be applied to procedures like those
      - That the identifications are worthless, since two of the five conditions are broken
      - That confidence taken at trial is the more reliable measurement of the two
    answer: 1
    explain: >-
      The authors say in their own summary that their conclusions about high-confidence
      identifications cannot yet be applied to jurisdictions without the reforms, which is the
      qualification most often dropped. No weakened version of the claim is offered. Calling the
      identifications worthless is a verdict the paper does not give. And a later statement is the
      one the claim excludes.
  - q: >-
      Which is the more surprising half of the finding, on this course's reading?
    options:
      - That an initial high-confidence identification made by an adult is remarkably accurate
      - That the relationship holds only when a lineup contains one suspect
      - That initial low confidence is a real warning, at about a coin toss in one estimate
      - That confidence expressed in court carries no information about accuracy at all
    answer: 2
    explain: >-
      Everybody expects a confident witness to be treated sceptically, and the estimate that an
      unsure one is about as likely to be wrong as right is the half nobody predicts. The
      high-confidence half is the one that gets reported. The one-suspect condition is one of five.
      And the paper does not say courtroom confidence carries nothing; it says its claim is about
      the initial statement.
  - q: >-
      Lesson 2's instrument and lesson 5's instrument have something in common. What?
    options:
      - Both were measured on samples too small to support any general claim
      - Both produce a number that is routinely reported as though it were a different number
      - Both were introduced in the 1880s and have not been revised since
      - Both turn on when the measurement was taken rather than only on what was measured
    answer: 3
    explain: >-
      Savings is time saved at relearning, which is a measurement at a stated delay, and the
      confidence claim is about the statement taken at the identification rather than later. Sample
      size is a real issue for one of them and not the other. Being misreported is true of savings
      and is not the shared property. And only one of the two is from the 1880s.

  # --- Techniques and transfer (L6) ---
  - q: >-
      An app teaches the method of loci and advertises that it will improve your memory for
      everything. Which part of that is supported by what this course read?
    options:
      - The technique's effect on ordered lists of arbitrary items, and not the rest
      - All of it, since the training study found durable improvement against an active control
      - None of it, since a study of about fifty people supports no claim at all
      - The claim about everything, provided the user practises for at least six weeks
    answer: 0
    explain: >-
      The evidence is about what the technique does to the material it is built for, and the step
      from that to everything is the transfer question this course has not answered for it. The
      durability finding is real and is about that material. A study of fifty people supports a
      narrow claim rather than none. And six weeks is the training length, not a licence.
  - q: >-
      Why does the training study's working-memory arm matter?
    options:
      - Because it lets the two kinds of training be compared directly for their size of effect
      - Because three arms give the study more statistical power than two would
      - Because it controls for the time, effort and expectation that any training involves
      - Because working memory is the system the method of loci is thought to act on
    answer: 2
    explain: >-
      An arm doing something else for similar time and effort is what separates the technique from
      the fact of being trained and tested, and it is the control whose absence emptied the
      far-transfer literature in Focus and Deep Work lesson 6. The direct comparison is a bonus.
      Power is not the reason. And no mechanism of that kind was read for this course.

  # --- Sorting a claim (L7) ---
  - q: >-
      "Your brain deletes memories you do not use, which is why revision works." Sort it, and say
      what this course adds to the sort.
    options:
      - Measurable, and the sample question asks how many people it was measured on
      - Two claims, and the sample question applies to the revision half rather than the deletion
      - False, and the sample question asks whether the study was large enough to show it
      - Unmeasurable as stated, and the sample question does not arise until something is measurable
    answer: 1
    explain: >-
      A mechanism and a recommendation are stuck together, and they need separate verdicts: the
      deletion claim defines nothing that could be counted, while the claim that revision works is
      measurable and How to Learn Anything reports evidence for it. Sorting the sentence as one
      claim, whichever way, throws away the half that holds.
