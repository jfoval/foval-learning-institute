*Fact-check (primary texts read: Sweller & Cooper 1985, Sweller et al. 2019, Dunlosky et al. 2013, Chi et al. 1989, Hmelo-Silver et al. 2007; bibliographic details checked against publisher/ERIC/JSTOR records)*
1. [high] "solve later problems in about half the time, with roughly a fifth of the errors" — secondary-source gloss (traces to a Matuschak note), not the paper. Exp. 3: 43.6 s vs 78.1 s (ratio 0.56), errors 0.18 vs 1.64 per problem (about one-ninth); Exp. 2: no significant time difference, errors 0.13 vs 0.50. Same figure is in SOURCES.md. — replace with the actual figures.
2. [high] "gave algebra students either problems to solve or the same problems presented as worked solutions" — design misdescribed; worked-example group got example-problem pairs (study then solve). — reword.
3. [high] Dunlosky's prior-knowledge condition attached to both self-explanation and elaborative interrogation, and Q4's "rate it moderate precisely because it needs prior knowledge" — Dunlosky attaches the moderator to EI only, says it is under-studied for self-explanation, and gives "evidence limited, not adequately evaluated in educational contexts" as the reason for the moderate rating. — correct scope and reason.
4. [high] Chi 1989 "physics students ... reread the example and copied it" — subjects were 8 university students with no college physics; the group difference was rereading during problem solving (searching for a line to use), not copying (Copy/Map episodes did not differ). "Chi's good students weren't cleverer readers" unverifiable (post-hoc split, ability not equated). — reword body, Q3 explain, source note.
5. [medium] "search and learning compete ... and search usually wins" cited to [7] — the competition claim is Sweller & Cooper 1985 p. 61–62; "search usually wins" is in neither. — cite [1][7], drop "usually wins".
6. [medium] "Since 2010" germane-load redefinition cited only to [7], which does not date it; the date is right (Sweller 2010, Educ Psychol Rev 22, 123–138). — add Sweller 2010 as a source.
7. [medium] "This is exactly where the worked-example advantage ran out in 1985" — 1985's dissimilar problems used the same operations in a different order; Stage 3 needs a new operation. — reword.
8. [low] Stage 2 "same moves in the same order" — Stage 2 has an extra divide step. — say so.
9. [low] Sample paragraph in a blockquote invites reading as a quotation; content is an accurate paraphrase and it is labelled "in the style of". — add "written for this lesson, not quoted".
10. [low] Highlighting hurts inference rests on one study (Peterson 1992) which Dunlosky says needs replication. — soften.
11. [low] "Both sides of the 2006 exchange accept that [worked examples beat problem-solving]" — Hmelo-Silver et al. accept scaffolding and fading but never endorse the worked-example effect. — reword.
12. [low] "about four chunks" uncited (Cowan 2001 via lesson 2). — acceptable as cross-reference; left as is.
