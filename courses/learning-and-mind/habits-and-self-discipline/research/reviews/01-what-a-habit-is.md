## 2026-09-19 — Lesson 01 — Stage 4, all passes, Tier A
**Reviewer:** one subagent in a fresh context, briefed with this course's research file and outline,
and with the eight review files from Time Management, the course this drafter finished immediately
before this one, so that recurring defects would be recognised rather than rediscovered.

**Verdicts as returned:** **depth FAIL**, **fact-check FAIL**, neutrality pass with fixes,
**pedagogy FAIL**, cold start pass with fixes, voice and media pass with fixes. **Send back.**

### Findings

1. **[critical] H1-01. Constructed material unlabelled, and footnote 5 unreachable.** The marker
   appeared zero times in the body and no sentence said the cases were constructed, while quiz items
   graded the reader on them. **Sixth recurrence of defect 20's compounding form across two courses.**
2. **[critical] H1-02. Outline decision 3 failed on the one source it was written for.** The
   paywalled Lally study's read level was in a footnote only, the body invented a recruitment detail
   ("who answered an advertisement") that nothing read records, and the figure carried no year.
3. **[critical] H1-03. A fabricated study setting used to mark a quiz answer wrong.** An explanation
   said the central study "was run in people's own homes". Nothing read says that.
4. **[critical] H1-04. An invented chain of custody.** The Sources block attributed the smoking-ban
   case to Orbell and Verplanken and then hedged against not having read them. **Nothing in the
   research file supports that attribution**, and the same entry claimed the body did not quote the
   case while the body quoted it. **The hedging made a fabrication read as scrupulousness**, which is
   the worst form this defect takes.
5. **[critical] H1-05. A quiz item with two defensible answers**, whose explanation named distractors
   by position and got the positions wrong, labelling the key as a distractor.
6. **[major] H1-06 to H1-11.** The mechanism the outline required, repetition in a consistent context
   through associative learning, was absent, which also undercut lesson 3; two of six quiz items
   applied the idea to a case not in the text, against a standard of half, and two reused the body's
   own cases; two figures with no marker, year or source; Go deeper described a section the course
   has not read; five unsourced generalisations about what most people find; and the Wood scope was
   dropped along with that paper's own criterion, **which is frequency-plus-context and therefore
   cuts against this course's decision 1**.
7. **[medium and low] H1-12 to H1-22.** An explanation conceding a time saving the lesson never
   claims; an asymmetric preview of the self-control dispute with the third position missing; the
   unread trade books characterised; "the case for is everything above" handing one side the whole
   lesson; an unverifiable cross-reference to Logic and Argument; six mechanically inserted
   contractions; one quiz item with only one absolute-free option; an exercise that dropped the third
   part of the lesson's own test; "automaticity" unglossed in a heading; four small additions beyond
   the sources; and one outline misconception never named.

### Clean on inspection
**All five prose quotations string-matched the research file character for character.** No arithmetic
to check. No trade-canon mention. Decision 1 held throughout, including in the distractors.
**Decision 6 handled well and deliberately**, which the reviewer singled out. Block grammar clean,
`minutes: 85` correct, both exercises executable from a blank page.

### Resolutions applied, same day

- **H1-04 first, because it is the worst.** The Orbell and Verplanken attribution is deleted: I
  invented it. The case is now credited to the paper that actually carries it, with a clause in the
  body saying that paper is reporting other researchers' work and that this course has not gone back
  to the study behind it. The footnote no longer claims the body does not quote what it quotes.
- **H1-01: fixed.** The runners and the three sorted cases carry an inline label with the marker on
  it, and footnote 5 now covers every constructed person in the lesson including those in the quiz.
- **H1-02, H1-03: fixed.** The read level for the paywalled study is in the body, the invented
  recruitment detail is gone, the year is there, and the quiz explanation now claims only what the
  study's own title claims.
- **H1-05: fixed.** The second key is rewritten so it is no longer true, and the explanation names
  every distractor by content.
- **H1-06: fixed, and it was the most useful finding.** The mechanism sentence gathered at Stage 1
  and dropped is now in the lesson, with the three words in it that do the work unpacked:
  *repetition*, *consistent context*, *associative learning*. Lesson 3's cue work depends on the
  middle one and lesson 1 had never established it.
- **H1-07 to H1-22: fixed**, including two quiz items re-cased onto people who are not in the body,
  the Wood criterion given with its scope and flagged as cutting against decision 1, the self-control
  preview given its third position, and the exercise's missing third test restored.
- **The Logic and Argument cross-reference is now to lesson 2**, on testing a definition by what it
  rules in and out, which that lesson does teach.

**Status after review:** clean. No second full pass: the trigger is wrong facts or a failed neutrality
check surviving, and neither does.

### What this review says about the pipeline
**Four of the five criticals are read-level discipline failing on the sources the course has read
least**, which is exactly where outline decision 3 predicted the risk and exactly where it was not
enough on its own. The drafter is careful with quotations, which string-matched perfectly again, and
careless in the sentences around them, which is `/draft-lesson`'s own measured note.

**The new thing here is that a fabrication was dressed in a hedge.** The invented attribution came
with "this course has not read that paper itself", which reads as scrupulous and was covering an
invention. **A reviewer should treat an unprompted hedge as something to check rather than as
reassurance**, and that is now in `/draft-lesson`'s defect list.
