## Scrutinise (Reviewer F: facts, neutrality, safety)
1. **Read levels.** Weger 2010, Weger 2014, Kluger 2024, DeMarree 2026: full text today (the gate
   closure has every figure and [V] string). PSPB 2017 and 2018: abstract only, said in the body.
   HBR: the article's description. Stanley 2000 and Gottman 2000 reply: abstracts. Please string-match
   each Sources read level against SOURCES and the gate closure.
2. **What each "active listening" condition contained and what it was compared with**: Weger 2010
   paraphrase against "O.K."/"That's great" with nonverbal involvement held constant; Weger 2014
   paraphrase plus requests to elaborate, against advice and against back-channels; Bodie 2015 is a
   regression of coded behaviours across 171 dyads, not a trained-versus-untrained comparison (the
   outline framed it as "41 trained against 130 untrained"; the lesson follows the paper); HBR
   texting and poor/moderate listeners; DeMarree learning goal against persuasion goal (not "an
   instruction to listen" against nothing), and the pilot's nonverbal-only control.
3. **Kluger's r̄**: never a percentage or a cause. The lesson gives the authors' own causal-leaning
   phrase ("a cause of superior performance") beside their caution ("only exposed the possibility");
   check that pairing is fair to them and not a gotcha. The chart's second point is job performance
   only (different sources or methods), not all outcomes; the caption says so and gives the .36
   all-effects figure for performance. Please redraw-check the SVG positions: x = 60 + 640r (.39 at
   310, CI 290 to 335; .21 at 194, CI 143 to 246).
4. **DeMarree**: the lesson says the manipulation failed by the authors' own check and that the test
   therefore says little about the idea. SOURCES Part C and the outline's summary ("telling one
   person to listen ... did not influence") are accurate to the abstract but miss this; check the
   lesson doesn't overcorrect into defending the programme. The pilot's d = 0.98 is given without a
   percentage conversion.
5. **Contested question 1, count test**: broad side gets PSPB 2017, 2018 and Kluger, one quotation
   (Kluger's "powerful and often overlooked predictor"); narrow side gets Weger 2010, 2014, Bodie 2015
