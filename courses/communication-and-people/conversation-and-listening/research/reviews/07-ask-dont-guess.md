### Findings

**R1 [major] Neutrality: Zaki fails the Turing test, and the section's "disagreement" is drawn where
the two largely agree.** Lines 427-431, 438-441, 471-474.
> "What they disagree on is whether the sharing of feeling is worth widening or worth setting aside
> for compassion." (440-441); "Zaki's reply, that empathy can be widened, is a serious position too"
> (473)

The section is titled "Is more empathy better?" and casts Zaki as the answer to Bloom on support.
On the same Edge page, in Zaki's own conversation, he says: "It's easy to overdose on empathy, and
empathy can be dangerous thing for an individual's well-being. It can cause you to burn out"; "The
idea that you can control empathy is not just meant so that everyone can turn their empathy up to
eleven all the time. It's just as important to know when to turn down one's empathy"; and, of his
wife's clinical work, "the last thing that any of her patients need if they're depressed, is for her
to be depressed as well. She needs to modulate her empathy". That is Bloom's Box 3 point about
support, made by Zaki. A reader who knows Zaki would say the lesson has him arguing for "more", when
his case is for *control*, up or down; the page's actual disagreement is Bloom's "empathy is a poor
guide to moral decision-making" against Zaki's "choose to align their empathy more with their
values", which is about moral choices, not about how to sit with a distressed friend. The counts
confirm the lean: Bloom is quoted 8 times in the two empathy sections and explained twice in the
course's voice ("it pulls you towards the one person you can picture"; "Bloom is not against caring,
and not against understanding"), Zaki 5 times with one line of explanation; Bloom has the last
authorial word (his Reality Club response; the four other respondents, including DeSteno's "Zaki is
right about empathy and compassion being partially subject to choice", are not mentioned, so "the
page carries no answer" reads as if Bloom's were the only response); and both misconceptions at 471-474
are about Bloom (one he argues against, one misreading him), with no misreading of Zaki named.
Secondary: Bloom's own case is also trimmed at its strongest point for close relationships. Box 3's
middle sentences, "The features of empathy that make it so worrisome in the policy domain ... might
not be problems when it comes to family and friends" and "while it might falter in a world of
strangers, it is well suited for more intimate interactions", are skipped between the two quoted
sentences at 417-420.
**Fix.** (a) Add to "Zaki's case", after 431: "He is just as clear that choice runs both ways: 'The
idea that you can control empathy is not just meant so that everyone can turn their empathy up to
eleven all the time. It's just as important to know when to turn down one's empathy', and he gives a
clinical example, a therapist who must not become depressed with a depressed patient."[5] (b) Rewrite
440-441: "What they disagree on is whether empathy, chosen well, is a good guide to moral decisions.
On support for someone in distress, both say the helper should not simply share the distress." (c)
Replace the second half of 473 with a Zaki misreading: "**'Zaki wants more empathy all round.'** He
argues for choosing it, and for turning it down when it would swamp you or the person you are

**R2 [major] A concealment finding among strangers applied to a case of half-display among
colleagues, with "very likely".** Lines 303-312, 317, 464-465; quiz 4 explanation (76-77).
> "What the studies suggest, on this course's reading, is that the colleague very likely did not see
> it. Perpetua was doing roughly what the tasters did, keeping a lid on a strong feeling ... Silence
> plus folded arms is not a message anyone reliably received." (308-312)

Perpetua sat "with her arms folded and said almost nothing for twenty minutes" and wants it seen.
That is closer to the *communicate* case, which the lesson itself says (294-296) the authors only
"suggest" from unread preliminary work, than to the tasters, who were instructed to keep a neutral
face. The observers in every study were strangers (or other students) guessing from a video or from
one statement; a colleague in a meeting is neither. "Very likely" and "not a message anyone reliably
received" are stated in the course's voice with no source (defect 22, third form). The same
overreach recurs as a finding at 317 ("a feeling someone is holding in mostly doesn't show") and 465
("a held-in feeling mostly doesn't show"): the studies show that concealed lies and concealed disgust,
in a lab, went undetected by strangers above chance; the authors' own General Discussion adds a
second precondition (the person must believe there is a route for leakage) and says "In many cases,
people may be well calibrated". **Fix.** Make Perpetua hide it (she keeps her face neutral and joins
in the discussion, and still leaves sure it showed), which matches the studies; or keep the folded
arms and write: "The studies tested people trying to hide a feeling from strangers, not a colleague
half-showing one to people who know her, so the carry-over is weaker here. On this course's reading
the colleague may well not have seen it, and the safe move for Perpetua is the same: if she wants it
known, she has to say it." Delete "Silence plus folded arms is not a message anyone reliably
received." At 317 and 465: "in these studies, a feeling people were hiding mostly went unseen by
strangers, so a calm face is weak evidence". Quiz 4 explanation: add "among strangers in a lab".
