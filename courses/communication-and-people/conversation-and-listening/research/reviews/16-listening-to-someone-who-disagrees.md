### F1. The misconception on listening instructions claims more than the study tested (Medium)

> **"If I try to listen well, they'll feel listened to."** In the four-lab test, an instruction to
> learn did not change how listened-to the partners felt.[15]

The lesson's own section says the people given the learning goal "did not report trying any harder,
so the authors say the idea was not really tested", and lesson 9 quotes the authors: "a failure of
the manipulation". A study in which people did not try harder cannot show that trying harder fails.
The misconception as worded is rebutted by evidence that doesn't bear on it.

**Fix (word-neutral):** **"Telling someone to listen better will make the other person feel heard."**
In the four-lab test, an instruction to learn changed neither how hard people said they tried nor how
listened-to their partners felt.[15]

### F2. "Both readings": each position misses its strongest sentence from the papers already read (Medium: selection, defect 14)

> **The effects are small, fragile, or about feelings rather than views.** This reading is built from
> the same papers, and their authors supply much of it: effects of 0.08 and 0.04 ...

The count is otherwise fair (Position A: [18][19] plus Braver Angels' own words; Position B: [16][18]
[19]; last word to the organisations' disclaimer and "what would settle it", no verdict; one
misconception against each side). But both sides are built at less than full strength from text the
course has read:

- **For A**, Kalla and Broockman's own counterweight to "small": "some may see these effects as
  relatively sizable given the null effects of many other door-to-door persuasion programs", and "few
  if any other campaign tactics have been rigorously shown to have lasting meaningful effects in the
  field on public opinion". Printing only the "small" half of a two-sided paragraph is the defect 9
  shape, even though it cuts against the lesson here.
- **For B**, the sceptic's two sharpest points are also in the read text: "a campaign implementing
  this approach should expect that a very large number of such conversations would be needed to
  produce detectable changes in aggregate public opinion" (Kalla and Broockman), and Baron et al.
  report "(mostly null) treatment effects on our pre-specified mechanisms" and on their secondary
  outcomes (support for pro-democratic politicians and actions, civil discourse). The 11-against-5
  minutes confound, now outside both positions, is also B material. As written, B is mostly the
  authors' concessions plus the retraction, which the lesson has just said "does not show the
  opposite".

**Fix:** one paraphrased clause each, so the balance holds. A: "...and the canvassing authors note that
few other campaign tactics have shown lasting effects at all." B: "...effects of 0.08 and 0.04, which
the canvassing authors say would need a very large number of conversations to move public opinion;
mostly null effects on the workshop trial's secondary outcomes; an explicit measure that just missed
significance; attitude effects that faded somewhat..." If either is quoted, append the strings to
SOURCES' lesson 16 gate closure as [V] first (decision 16). Consider moving the duration sentence into
B to pay for the words.

### F3. All the persuasion evidence runs in one direction, and the lesson doesn't say so (Medium: neutrality)

> The campaigns aimed to move voters towards one side of two contested questions, one about
> immigration policy and one about transgender people. This course takes no side on either

The retracted study and all three canvassing experiments sought change in the same direction, and the
canvassing partners were advocacy organisations (the paper's first note). A reader on the right can
fairly conclude from the lesson that "changing minds by listening" has been shown only for one side's
causes; a reader on the left may equally want to know whether the method is neutral technology. The
authors' "both sides" question gestures at this but is phrased as a future-consequences question, not
as a limit on what the trials show. SOURCES has no trial run in the other direction.

**Fix (about 30 words):** after "takes no side on either": "All three campaigns, like the retracted
study, pushed in the same direction, and this course has read no trial of the method used for the
other side, so whether it works as well in reverse is untested." The later "both sides" sentence can
then shorten.

### F4. The "not talking at all" position is stated thinly, and the only material beside it reads as a reply (Medium: Turing test)

> Some people hold that sitting down to listen to certain views gives them a standing they should
> not have.

A holder would recognise "standing" (legitimacy) but not the whole view: its usual second limb is the
cost to the people the views are about, who are asked to treat their own dignity as a debating
question. The bullet then gives its remaining space to Kalla and Broockman's point, whose final clause
("may need to balance ... conditions in which people do not feel threatened") argues toward openness,
so the position is described in one clause and followed by a counterweight. The lesson honestly says
no primary statement was read, which is right (defect 10).

**Fix:** "Some people hold that sitting down to listen to certain views gives them a standing they
should not have, and asks the people those views are about to bear the cost." And reorder the
Kalla and Broockman sentence so the Paluck half (norms against exclusionary behaviour did reduce its
consequences) comes last, which is the half that supports the position.

### F5. LAPP is Doherty's advice in a reprinted feature, not an organisational method (Low: attribution)

> puts "Acknowledge what you heard" second in its four steps
> Now take it through Braver Angels' four steps, which it calls **LAPP**

The page introduces the list as "advice from William Doherty, a marriage and family therapist and
co-founder of Braver Angels" in a "Tips For Talking With Someone With Different Views" box, then "Try
the LAPP Technique". It is hosted by Braver Angels, so the organisation evidently endorses it, but the
page does not present it as the organisation's four steps.

**Fix:** "the four steps its co-founder William Doherty calls LAPP"; in the opening bullet, "Braver
Angels' co-founder puts 'Acknowledge what you heard' second in four steps". Quiz 6 and the objective
can keep "Braver Angels' LAPP steps" once the body has said whose they are. (The page also supports
the "Reds and Blues" gloss directly: "conservative (or 'red' in the group's lexicon) and liberal
('blue')", so [5] can be added there.)

### F6. Three small misdescriptions of the canvassing experiments (Low)

1. "The two later experiments found d = 0.08 and 0.04." Experiments 2 and 3 ran in 2016, before
   Experiment 1 (autumn 2018), and Experiment 2 used narratives on video. **Fix:** "The other two
   experiments, on transgender people (one with stories shown on video, one by phone), found..."
2. Position A: "the canvassing experiments, in which argument alone did nothing". Only Experiment 1
   had an arguments-only arm. **Fix:** "the first canvassing experiment, in which argument alone did
   nothing".
3. Position A: "the exchange of stories made the difference". The authors say removing it
   "significantly reduces if not eliminates" the effect and that their experiments "cannot isolate a
   particular mechanism". **Fix:** "which the authors judge was mainly responsible".

### F7. "None changes whether a result held" is the course's judgement, and one figure wasn't confirmed (Low)

> for the results this lesson uses, the listed differences are small ... and none changes whether a
> result held.[8]

True for 0.29/0.31, −0.08/−0.09, 0.23/0.24 and t 7.3/7.4. But the report states "The
reproducibility audit also did not cover the reproducibility of the inferential conclusions reported
in the paper", and for the 17.2%/12.6% attrition it says only that the computing script was found and

fair as the reports' framing and needs no change.)

**Fix:** "...and, on this course's reading, none changes whether a result held; the report itself did
not assess the conclusions."
