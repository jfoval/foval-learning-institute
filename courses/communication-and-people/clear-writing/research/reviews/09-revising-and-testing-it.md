### Resolutions

1. F1 — fixed. The paraphrase is demoted to body text with only Sommers's own words inside
   quotation marks. **This is the finding to remember from the round**: the wrapper came from the
   research file, which is normally the thing that protects a lesson, so a drafter reading it in
   good faith can lift a paraphrase and quote it. Quote only what sits inside quotation marks in
   `SOURCES.md`.
2. F2, F4, F6 — fixed together by dropping to what the file records and being open about the rest.
   The lesson keeps the one recorded quotation, states the guide's scoping honestly (short pages
   and survey questions, usability testing for anything longer), and says in terms that steps 4 and
   5 are **this course's own wording rather than quotations**. Nothing is now attributed to a page
   at a read status the research file doesn't support.
3. F3, F5 — fixed, and the fix is better teaching than the claim was. A callout headed "Six to nine
   people, and what to do when you have one" says the guidance works with six to nine and is right
   that six teaches you more, then gives the course's advice **as a judgement and labels it one**:
   do it with one rather than not at all, because the step people skip is the first one. The
   misconception entry now says what one reader buys you and what it doesn't.
4. F7, F8 — fixed. "most writing advice skips" replaces the universal claim, and a paragraph after
   the method says fifteen writers can't establish a fact about two populations and that the
   finding is a distinction to look for in your own drafts.
5. D1 — fixed, and this is the largest addition. **A paraphrase test is now written out in full**:
   a council tax passage, a reader's verbatim paraphrase that is fluent, confident and eighty
   percent right, the diagnosis of which sentence lost the deadline (lesson 4's subject-verb gap
   and lesson 5's stress position, both named), the rewrite that follows from the diagnosis rather
   than from taste, and the second reader's paraphrase showing the test pass. A `:::predict`
   follows it on a third reader who understands the document perfectly and still intends to ignore
   it, which teaches the distinction between a wording finding and a content finding. That is the
   outline's example (a), and the predict carries part of (b).
6. D2 — fixed. A paragraph after Sommers's rejection of the linear stage theory says what each
   claim is for: hers describes how composing happens, "revise before you edit" is advice about a
   draft already in front of you, and a reader who finds themselves revising while drafting is
   doing what her experienced writers did.
7. D3 — fixed, four items, ending with the one that beats the other three.
8. D4 — fixed. A new section maps what each of the nine lessons did to the one document the reader
   has carried, links the project by name, and ends by telling them to send the document to the
   person it was always for. The course now has an ending.
9. P1 — fixed. Item 3 is rebuilt on the content-versus-wording distinction, using a case that
   appears only in the new predict block.
10. P2 — noted, not fixed here: the exercise and the project's Part 4 overlap deliberately, and the
    new closing section now names the project so the relation is visible. If John reads both and
    finds the duplication irritating, the fix belongs in the assessment rather than the lesson.
11. V1, V3 — fixed. 97 contractions, morals folded in, and two machine sentences rewritten.
12. V2 — fixed. "Allow forty minutes for this" and `minutes: 80`, measured.
13. P3 — **re-measured after every change, because eight rounds have shown the rewrite breaking
    it.** It held: indices B D A C B D, all four used, no adjacent repeat, "always longest" 17
    percent, "always shortest" 0 percent.
14. V4, V5 — the think-block count is now three, and the figure is **declined**. Sommers's coding
    scheme is a real candidate, but the lesson gained a long worked example this round and the grid
    would be decoration beside it. Recorded rather than silently skipped.

**CW-06, closed with reasoning rather than with a line.** The build fails a lesson citing a work
that `SOURCES.md` marks unread, but only if the file carries a machine-readable
`<!-- unread: ... -->` line. This course has none, which is why nothing caught lesson 6's White
attribution. **The line was not added, and the reason matters.** Williams is named twelve times
across five lessons, every one of them disclosing that the course has not read him, which is
correct practice. The check cannot tell a disclosed mention from an undisclosed citation, so the
line would fail the build on five lessons doing it right and would need five exceptions, leaving a
check that is green for the wrong reason. `scripts/CLAUDE.md` says a check that is easy to route
around is worse than no check, and a warning nobody can act on trains everyone to ignore the
output. Recorded in `course-wide.md` as a limitation of the check rather than a gap in the course.
