## 2026-09-09, lesson 8, targeted fairness check (Part 3.2)

Run during drafting rather than at Stage 4, because `research/OUTLINE.md` flagged this lesson as the
one that "could easily become a hit piece or an apologia" and set an explicit test: **would a reader
who loves *The Elements of Style* finish it feeling fairly treated?** One reviewer, fresh context,
with the standards, the research file and both primary texts.

**Verdict on the draft: passed the Strunk side of the test and failed the Pullum side.** The reviewer's
summary is the finding worth keeping: *"A reader who loves the book finishes feeling fairly treated,
comfortably. A reader who found Pullum's essay convincing finishes thinking he was careless about a
paragraph he actually quoted, and never meets the argument he built his case on."*

That is the failure mode nobody predicted. The outline warned about a hit piece. The draft was an
apologia, and a quiet one, because every individual sentence in it was accurate.

### Findings, and what was done

**F1 (severe). The lesson's central reveal was something Pullum says out loud.** The draft presented
Strunk's 1918 qualification about the passive as a discovery made by opening the primary text.
Pullum's essay quotes that same qualification, calls the advice around it "fairly sensible", and
grants the topic-dependence point the draft also treated as a find. A reader would have finished
believing he missed a paragraph he reproduced and credited. **Fixed:** his paragraph is now quoted in
full, and the section says plainly that anyone who claims he overlooked the moderation has not read
him. The section's purpose changed from a reveal to a comparison of two readings of one page.

**F2 (severe). The split verdict was awarding Pullum the half of his case he disclaimed.** He writes
that the tutors' overinterpretation "is not what I am most concerned about here", and names his real
concern as the authors' competence. The draft conceded the tutors and disputed the competence.
**Fixed:** that sentence is now quoted in its own callout, and the closing assessment addresses the
charge he actually made.

**F3 (severe). His strongest argument was absent.** His case does not rest mainly on the four
misdiagnosed examples; it rests on the book breaking its own rules on the same page, including
"Write with nouns and verbs" followed immediately by "The adjective hasn't been built that can pull a
weak or inaccurate noun out of a tight place." **Fixed:** that evidence is now quoted as "part two,
and this is the stronger half", with the honest note that it comes from White's 1959 text, which this
course has not read and therefore cannot check.

**F4 (severe). The charge was restated more strongly than written, then refuted in that form.** Pullum
hedges with "presumably fingered as passive". The draft refuted "the authors could not tell a passive
from an adjective". **Fixed:** the charge is quoted verbatim with the hedge intact, and the response
now says the two readings are both available from the page.

**F5 (moderate). The specimen superstition was Strunk's own, and the lesson did not say so.** The
draft used "never begin a sentence with *however*" as its example of an untested prohibition while
twice redirecting charges to White. 1918 contains that rule in its glossary. **Fixed:** quoted and
attributed.

**F6 (moderate). Opening the text corrects Pullum too, and the draft omitted it.** He writes that
"Strunk's original version never even mentioned split infinitives." The 1918 glossary has an entry.
**Fixed:** it is now quoted under a heading called "The finding that cuts the other way", and the
practice exercise ends by requiring the learner to report findings that go against them, citing this.

**F7 (moderate). A stitched quotation presented as continuous.** Three separate bulleted paragraphs of
Pullum's *none* evidence had been merged inside one set of quotation marks, with the *Dracula*
quotation truncated unmarked. **Fixed:** rewritten as prose that reports his three searches, with each
quotation given in full.

**F8 (moderate). A quiz explanation was factually wrong.** It claimed three distractors named material
in Strunk's original; two of them are White's. **Fixed:** the item was rewritten, and the new
explanation names the split infinitive and *however* trap explicitly.

**F9 (moderate). No living defender of the book had a voice.** The pro-book case was carried entirely
by Strunk's self-description and Pullum's concessions, which is the position stated in its opponent's
words. **Fixed:** a paragraph now gives the defenders' case in their terms and says nothing below
refutes it. The Sources note now records that White's defenders are unheard as well as his book unread.

**F10 to F12 (minor), all fixed.** An assertion in the lesson's voice ("producing a generation who
red-circle every instance of *be*") that is anecdote in the essay, now attributed. A line congratulating
the course on its own honesty, cut. An ellipsis that removed Pullum's reason and then supplied it as
the lesson's own gloss, restored to him. A "what people get wrong" entry was added saying that
following these rules does not make anyone a fool, since most people were taught them by someone they
trusted.

**Quiz pedagogy, fixed.** Answer index 0 was never used across six items; two keys were the longest
option, one by 16 characters, and both were guessable from the course's evident posture. Now: indices
3, 1, 0, 2, 1, 2, no adjacent repeat, all four used, every spread within 7 characters, no key longest
or conspicuously shortest.

### What was not changed, and why

The reviewer verified every other quotation as exact and found no em dashes. Two of its incidental
notes were checked and are already handled correctly in the lesson: 1918's fourth disputed pair is
about a guitar rather than a rooster, and 1918 reads "he was very sorry" where Pullum quotes "she",
which is itself evidence that Pullum is working from White's text.

**The general lesson for the rest of this course.** The draft failed in the direction opposite to the
one the outline predicted, and every sentence in it was true. Fairness failed at the level of
selection: which of an opponent's arguments get quoted, which half of their case gets answered, and
whose discovery a finding is presented as. A checker that only verifies quotations would have passed
it.

## 2026-09-11 — Lesson 08 — second Stage 4 round, Tier A
**Reviewer:** one subagent, fresh context, all passes including the mandatory neutrality pass.
Given `course-wide.md` and the round above, and asked whether the earlier findings held.

**The earlier round's twelve findings were all applied and nothing regressed.** The reviewer
verified each one in place. The quiz repair from that round also held.

**Verdict on this draft:** every one of 22 Pullum quotations and 5 Strunk quotations was diffed
against the fetched primary texts and every one is exact. It failed the neutrality pass anyway, in
the same way and for the same reason: not in what it says, in what it selects.

**CW checklist:** CW-01 clean. CW-02 defects 1, 2, 3 and 5 present; 4 and 6 clean. CW-04 clean.
CW-05 clean, and this is the first lesson in the course where every number checked out first time.
CW-06 still open at course level.

### Findings

**Neutrality, the main event**

The reviewer counted before reading for tone, which is what the command asks for, and the counts
are the finding. **Pullum: 22 direct quotations, across six sections, his argument reconstructed in
his own structure, his method given a section of its own, his concessions given a dedicated
callout. Strunk: 5 quotations, two of them the book's aim statement, two of them glossary entries
used as evidence against him.** Pullum is explained; Strunk is quoted and adjudicated. The last
word in the closing assessment goes to Pullum, and so does the last word in the misconception list.

1. [severe] **N1.** Strunk's best card is asserted twice and never shown. `SOURCES.md` calls his
   1918 statement of the topic-position principle "the most useful single fact in this whole file".
   The lesson said "its own author states the topic-position principle in 1918" and a quiz
   explanation said "Strunk says the same thing in 1918", with no text behind either, while the
   one place a reader met the qualification was **inside a Pullum quotation**. Pullum's parallel
   illustration was quoted in full. The Sources note claimed the lesson used "the Restoration
   dramatists pair"; the word Restoration appeared nowhere in the body.
2. [severe] **N2.** The counter-finding that cuts *for* Pullum was missing while the one that cuts
   against him had its own heading and eleven lines. Strunk's rule 3 contains "two statements
   **which** might have been made independently", a restrictive *which*, which is the construction
   the later rule forbids. Verified in the 1918 text.
3. [severe] **N3.** The lesson's load-bearing premise, "A rule contradicted by the usage of good
   writers of its own period is not a rule of English", stated as established. Under 3.1 that is a
   definitional commitment of the descriptive school. The prescriptive reply, that a standard
   variety is maintained by convention and edited practice, was never stated in terms its holders
   would accept, and a quiz explanation dismissed two alternatives as substituting "authority for
   evidence, which is the habit the lesson exists to break".
4. [moderate] **N4.** Loaded framing in the lesson's own voice, including "sometimes with the
   suggestion that a person who breaks them is careless".
5. [moderate] **N5.** The misconception entry covering *however*, *but* and *and* said
   "Contradicted by counting" when only *however* had been counted, in a lesson whose whole
   argument is that rules without evidence are superstition.
6. [moderate] **N6.** Henry James's 1:15 ratio runs toward the rule and was reported and then
   converted into a debunking, teaching the reader to read a split result as a refutation.

**Fact-check**

8. [moderate] **F2.** "its most famous maxim is quoted approvingly in lesson 6 of this course."

### Resolutions

1. N1 — fixed, and it is the most important change in this round. Strunk's Restoration passage is
   now quoted in full, in his own words, **before** the argument starts, with the observation that
   both his sentences are passive so the choice he describes is about which word gets to be the
   subject. The lesson says plainly that this is the strongest card the book has and is easy to
   miss when the argument gets loud. Pullum's version now follows it rather than standing in for
   it. The Sources claim is true as a result.
2. N2 — fixed. Strunk's own restrictive *which* from rule 3 is quoted in the predict answer, with
   the point that the founding text breaks the rule its revision added, in the course of explaining
   relative clauses. The section that reports findings cutting one way now reports one cutting the
   other.
3. N3 — fixed, and step 4 of the method is better for it. It now names both schools, states the
   descriptive premise as this course's position rather than as a fact, gives the prescriptive case
   a fair paragraph ("it isn't silly... they aren't fools"), and then says what a count actually
   settles: the narrow question of whether the rule describes how careful writers wrote. Whether to
   follow it anyway is separated out as a question the numbers don't touch.
4. N4, N5, N6 — fixed. "with real conviction behind them" replaces the careless line; the
   misconception entry now covers only *however* and says in terms that this course has **not**
   counted *but* and *and* and isn't going to pronounce on them.
5. F1 — fixed. Both sentences restored, with a line naming this as the third thing usually cut and
   noting it is the one place Pullum praises White.
6. F2, F3, F4, F5 — fixed. Lesson 6 is described correctly. Gopen and Swan's caveat is now in the
   body, in a new opening passage that teaches objective 1 directly: a description can be wrong and
   that is its virtue, a prohibition offers nothing to check, and their own "None of these should
   be considered 'rules.'" is what a description looks like when its authors are honest.
7. D1 — fixed, four items, including Williams, which Pullum himself recommends in place of
   *Elements* and which the lesson had omitted.
8. D2 — fixed by the same new passage as F3.
9. P1 — fixed. Two items rebuilt on rules the body never mentions: ending a sentence with a
   preposition, and a museum leaflet where the passive is right. Re-measured, and the rewrite put
   two keys on adjacent items; one moved. Final: indices D B A B D C, all four used, no adjacent
   repeat, 0 percent longest, 17 percent shortest.
10. V1, V2 — fixed. 77 contractions in the lesson's own voice, and `minutes: 80`, measured. The
    cue took two attempts: "Give this about thirty minutes" matches nothing, because the tool wants
    its cue word immediately followed by the number. "Allow thirty minutes for this" works.
11. V3, V4 — fixed together. A **table** now carries the three-way attribution: five charges, whose
    text each lands on, and the verdict from the primary text, with a line pointing out that this
    is exactly the two-dimensional content lesson 7 says a table is for. A `:::checkpoint` follows
    it, asking what is wrong and what is right about "Strunk and White were so incompetent they
    couldn't even identify a passive", which is the sentence a reader will actually meet.
