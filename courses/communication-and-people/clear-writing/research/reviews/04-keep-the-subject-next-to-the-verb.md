# Reviews: Writing Clearly, lesson 4 — Keep the subject next to the verb

## 2026-09-10 — Lesson 04 — depth, fact-check, pedagogy, voice and media, Tier A
**Reviewer:** one subagent, fresh context, all passes. Given `course-wide.md` first.

**Verdict on the draft:** send back. A lesson whose whole method is "replace the judgement with a
count" got four of its own counts wrong, including the gap in its centrepiece worked example.
Five of CW-02's six defects present, and Gopen and Swan trimmed for the third lesson running.

**CW-01 clean:** no 130-word figure.

### Findings

**Fact-check** (taken first, because this is where the lesson failed)

1. [highest] **F1.** "Subject: the new supplier. Verb: has missed. Gap: twenty-two words." The
   interruption is **18 words**. Counted. The lesson's entire claim is that a count beats an
   impression, and it miscounts the sentence it invites the reader to count.
2. [major] **F2.** The lesson says "their wording is worth having exactly" and then trims the
   quotation. The source reads: "Anything of length that intervenes between subject and verb is
   read as an interruption, **and therefore as something of lesser importance.**" The dropped
   clause is not decorative. It is the emphasis claim that the three-fixes section depends on, so
   the lesson cut the half that does its own work later. Verified against the full text.
3. [major] **F3.** "None of these reader-expectation principles should be considered 'rules.'"
   absent for the third lesson running, though `SOURCES.md` says it must survive into the lessons.
4. [major] **F4.** Three more miscounts. "Thirty-eight words" of a 36-word sentence. "Nineteen
   words" of an 18-word sentence. "fourteen words of vagueness" of 15. All counted.
5. [medium] **F5.** "You just got the verb four words in instead of twenty-six." The verb of the
   original lands at word 28. Twenty-six matches neither counting convention.
6. [medium] **F6.** Quiz item 1 said "the twenty word wait"; the gap in its own stem is 19.
7. [minor] **F7.** Williams is asserted to be "the fuller version of this material" before the
   disclosure that the course has not read him.
8. Verified, no action: GOV.UK's "try to split up sentences that are over 25 words long" is
   verbatim; the opening's "Twenty-four words later" is correct; all three fixes in the worked
   example do demonstrate what they claim, and each genuinely moves what lands last.

**Depth**

9. [major] **D1.** "There is no magic threshold, and anyone who gives you one is selling
   something. But as rough guidance: under about five words is invisible, five to ten is usually
   fine, ten to twenty is where readers start to notice, and past twenty you are asking for real
   work." Four invented bands, one sentence after disclaiming exactly that. Nothing in
   `SOURCES.md` supports any of them. This is CW-01's shape: a specific figure that reads as
   sourced and gets carried forward by later lessons.
10. [medium] **D2.** "This is why the cost is not flat... Twenty-four words cost a great deal." A
    rising cost curve is a claim about memory load, with no source and no label saying it is
    reasoning rather than a finding.
11. [major] **D3.** No "Go deeper". Fourth lesson running.
12. [medium] **D4.** 2,241 words against an outline that asks for what the teaching needs and
    notes the courses at standard run five to eight thousand. Not padding, the reverse. The
    outline also specified a 22-word interruption for the worked example; the lesson built an
    18-word one and then labelled it 22.

**Neutrality**

13. None found, and the pass is not needed: the lesson makes no political, religious or value
    claims. The one craft disagreement it touches, sentence length, is handled correctly, with
    GOV.UK's 25-word rule presented as a rule of thumb rather than settled by preference.

**Pedagogy**

14. [medium] **P1.** Two of six items were phrase-recognition rather than application. Item 5's
    key restated a body sentence almost word for word; item 6's key restated the callout.
15. [minor] **P2.** Adjacent items 1 and 2 shared answer index D.
16. [minor] **P3.** No before-and-after rewrite pair anywhere in the quiz, on a lesson entirely
    about rewriting. The transfer test would be better served by one.
17. [minor] **P4.** Only two think-while-reading blocks, against three in lessons 1 and 2 and four
    in lesson 3.
18. [good] **P5.** Option shapes measured: "always longest" 17 percent, "always shortest" 33
    percent in the draft, both under the pass mark, and every key correct with every `explain`
    naming the right letter. CW-02's sixth defect was not present in the draft.

**Voice and media**

19. [major] **V1.** Zero contractions in 2,467 words.
20. [minor] **V2.** Two "Here is" openers.
21. [minor] **V3.** "You have now met two facts about readers that both turn out to be about the
    same thing." Textbook recap voice.

23. [major] **V5.** The chart's viewBox was 520 wide, so **eight of its ten labels rendered at

**Block grammar**

25. Clean. Four blocks, correct fences, blank lines throughout, no nesting, CW-04 not present.

### Resolutions

1. F1, F4, F5, F6 — all six counts recomputed from the sentences themselves and corrected: the
   worked example's gap is **eighteen**, the wrinkle's pair is **thirty-six and eighteen** with a
   **fifteen**-word gap, the opening rewrite gets its verb "four words in instead of
   twenty-eight", and quiz item 1 says **nineteen**. The worked example now adds "Count them,
   because that's the method and it's worth doing once on a sentence somebody else wrote", which
   turns the fix into a use of the lesson's own tool. **Per CW-01's closing rule, the whole course
   was grepped for the old figures afterwards**; nothing outside this lesson carried them.
2. F2 — fixed. The quotation is restored in full, and a paragraph after it draws out the second
   clause, that the reader files an interruption as the lesser thing. The three-fixes section now
   names that as the reason the fixes are not interchangeable, so the restored half earns its keep
   rather than sitting there.
3. F3 — fixed, in the same place, with the same wording this course now uses every time it quotes
   them.
4. D1 — fixed the way lesson 2 handled its unresearched claims, by disclosure rather than
   deletion. The bands move into a callout headed "There is no threshold, and here is the honest
   version of one", which says they are the drafter's working rule of thumb reasoned from the
   principle, not findings, and that the number that matters is the one per sentence rather than
   the boundary. Repeated in Sources.
5. D2 — fixed by naming it: "that rising cost is this course's reasoning from Gopen and Swan's
   'interruption', not a measured curve from a study".
6. D3 — fixed. Four items, including Gopen's *Expectations* for the book-length version.
7. F7 — fixed: "reported to be the fuller treatment", and the Sources entry now says no claim
   about the book's contents goes beyond that report.
8. P1, P2, P3 — fixed together by rebuilding items 2 and 5. **Item 2 is now the before-and-after
   rewrite item P3 asked for**: a grant letter with an 18-word gap, its rewrite, and the question
   of whether the rewrite did the job, where the answer is yes for the gap and no for the
   emphasis. Item 5 is now a subeditor-and-author case that tests the same idea on a situation the
   body never describes. That also moved item 2's key off D, so the adjacent repeat is gone:
   indices are D B A B C B, all four used, no adjacent repeat.
9. P4 — fixed. A third block, a `:::predict` before the three fixes are revealed, asking the
   reader to derive the set of fixes from the shape of the problem rather than be handed a list.
10. P5 — re-measured after the rewrite, and the rewrite had introduced the defect the draft did
    not have. "Always pick the shortest" reached **67 percent**, one item short of the pass mark,
    because the new keys were written tighter than their distractors. Two keys lengthened; final
    measurement "always longest" 17 percent, "always shortest" 33 percent. **This is the second
    lesson running where the rewrite created the tell**, which is why CW-02 now says to measure
    after, not before.
11. V1, V2, V3 — fixed. 52 contractions, both "Here is" openers rewritten, and the recap sentence
    is now "Two facts about readers, and they turn out to be one fact."
12. V4 — fixed. The exercise opens "Allow 45 minutes for this", which uses a cue word the script
    knows, and `minutes: 75`, measured.
13. V5, V6 — fixed. The chart is redrawn at viewBox 420, so its smallest label renders at about
    12px on a phone, with every coordinate re-laid-out and checked for collisions. A line in the
    body now refers to it and says which sentence it draws. Read by eye at desktop width in the
    dark theme.
14. D4 — **partly declined.** The lesson is now 3,064 words rather than 2,241, and the growth is
    the restored quotation, the disclosure callout, the third think-block, the Go deeper and the
    rebuilt quiz items, all of which are teaching rather than padding. It is still short of the
    outline's five to eight thousand. Padding it to reach a number would be the wrong move, and
    `docs/QUEUE.md` already records the standing judgement that this course carries its depth in
    exercises. **Flagged for John**: if he reads this lesson and finds it thin, the fix is more
    worked examples, and the obvious candidate is a second full worked sentence in the wrinkle
    section. The outline's 22-word specification was not restored, because rebuilding the example
    would mean rewriting three dependent passages for a gain of four words.
