## 2026-09-24 — Final test and project — Stage 4, one reviewer, fresh context

**How it was read.** Every item's key was worked from its stem against the lesson text it depends
on, with the lesson open, and every explanation was read against the option its key selects. One
caveat on method: the test file was opened with its keys visible, so "answer before reading the key"
was done as "rework from the stem and the lesson", not blind. Lessons 6 to 10 were read in their
current state during the concurrent neutrality audit, and every quotation in the test was then
string-matched against the lessons and `SOURCES.md` after the fixes (all match; the only unmatched
quoted strings are the invented headlines and posts in the stems).

**Verdicts.** **Facts pass with fixes**: every key is right by what the lessons teach; one
explanation attributed a classification to lesson 4 that lesson 4 doesn't make (F1). **Pedagogy fail,
now fixed**: the mechanical shape checks passed, but the test could largely be passed by picking the
calibrated or "contested" option (P1), and four items copied a lesson's own case, checkpoint or quiz
item (P2 to P5). **Neutrality pass with one fix** (N1). **Safety pass**: no item asks readers about
their own weight or food, every item touching weight is about a third party or a headline, items 24
and 29 send medicine questions to a professional, and the callout is verbatim in both files; the
project lacked lesson 11's check on eating-less claims (S1). **Project pass with fixes** (J1 to J7).
**Voice pass**: no em or spaced en dashes, no banned words; contractions 14 per 1,000 in the test
body and 12 in the project; project bold 21 per 1,000.

Allocation: L1 3, L2 2, L3 2, L4 2, L5 2, L6 4, L7 3, L8 4, L9 4, L10 2, L11 2 = 30. The outline's
blueprint ("about three per lesson, four for 6, 8 and 9") sums to 36; the drafter kept 30 and gave
lessons 2, 3, 4, 5, 10 and 11 two each, which matches the test's own introduction. Accepted.

### Findings

**Pedagogy**
- **P1 (high), the whole test.** Measured: option spreads 1 to 13 characters, key sole-longest in
  0 of 30, positions A 8, B 7, C 7, D 8 with no adjacent repeat, and `check-quiz-shape.cjs` passes.
  By judgement, though, "pick the balanced or calibrated option" scored about 19 of 30 (63%, close
  to the 70% pass mark): in items 2, 3, 7 to 11, 13 to 16, 20, 21, 23, 25, 26 and 28 to 30 the key was
  the one moderate answer among extremes ("Settled for...", "Nothing", "worthless", "proves"). And
  "contested" appeared in six options across the test, five of them keys (7, 11, 16, 21, 28), so it
  worked as a tell. Mental Fitness's P1, again.
- **P2 (high), item 30.** A near copy of lesson 11's quiz item 6: a reader, a contested finding,
  and a key of "a premise of her own ... write down", with the same "wait until settled" and "defer to
  the guide" distractors.
- **P3 (medium), item 13.** It restated lesson 6's checkpoint ("The mainstream and the dissent agree
  on two links of the chain. Name them, and the step where they part") with the chain spelled out in
  the stem.
- **P4 (medium), item 1.** Lesson 1's exercise study 1 with new numbers (300 bowel cancer patients
  and processed meat, here 400 stomach cancer and pickled vegetables), and a key that was that
  exercise's checkpoint answer.
- **P5 (medium), item 29.** Lesson 11's own example, "eat more leafy greens" for someone on warfarin,
  with the same NHS quotations as the key.
- **P6 (low), noted and not changed.** Item 4 applies lesson 2's "One catch" paragraph (dividing by
  0.72); item 5 is lesson 2's cereal checkpoint with the premise fixed; item 8 is Look AHEAD's shape
  and says so; items 12 and 15 mirror Cochrane's headline and its low-bias footnote; item 17 is
  lesson 7's SSaSS misconception with magnesium in place of potassium; item 19's conflict-of-interest
  distractor echoes lesson 8's quiz item 4. Each puts new numbers and people on a lesson idea and
  names the lesson case in its explanation, which is application of the kind the outline allows.
- **P7 (low), item 7.** "differ only in stress" read as psychological stress.
- **P8 (low), noted and not changed.** Lesson 6's fourth objective (relative to absolute effect by
  baseline risk) has no item in the test. Lesson 6's quiz item 3 tests it, lesson 7's quiz item 1 and
  item 10's arithmetic sit near it, and the test already gives lesson 6 four items.

**Facts**
- **F1 (medium), item 9.** The explanation said "Lesson 4 read WHO's fact sheet this way: that
  'Dietary and physical activity patterns are largely the result of environmental and societal
  conditions' is an empirical claim". Lesson 4 calls the responsibility sentence a value judgement
  and the "chronic, relapsing disease" sentence a mix of empirical claim and classification; it
  never sorts the patterns sentence.
- **F2 (low), item 28.** "The genetic studies found no protection against stroke" understates
  Millwood, where stroke risk rose steadily with genetically predicted drinking.
- **F3 (low), item 10.** "(The label and figures are invented.)" The RDA 11 mg and UL 40 mg happen to
  be a real mineral's adult male figures; nothing in the course teaches them, so the claim of
  invention is the only problem.
- **F4 (low), item 12.** "Fewer heart attacks and strokes are heart outcomes": strokes aren't.

**Neutrality**
- **N1 (medium), item 14.** A diet trial finds less benefit per unit of LDL than the genetic studies
  predict; the key said "It fits, since the effect appears to grow with years of exposure", as the
  reading "lesson 6's evidence supports". Lesson 6 also sets out the dissent's reading, Astrup's
  larger particles against the EAS panel's particle-number proviso, which explains the same shortfall
  in a diet trial. Keying only the mainstream's reading as what the evidence supports failed the
  count lesson 6 itself passes.
- **N2 (low), item 21.** The explanation said "'tiny' isn't only opinion, since effect sizes can be
  measured", without the half lesson 6 teaches: where small becomes too small to act on is a
  threshold, "a value laid on the evidence".
- **N3 (low), item 25, not changed.** The key follows lesson 9's own sentence that the process
  question is "partly about evidence and mostly about who should decide". A supporter of the new
  process would say its case was the committee report's quality, which is evidence. That is lesson
  9's framing, not the test's; the concurrent audit of lesson 9 is the place for it.
- Perspectives checked against: saturated fat mainstream (AHA, WHO, Sun and Hu) and dissent (Astrup,
  Ramsden, the Scientific Foundation); both salt camps; Lustig against Kahn and Sievenpiper; NutriRECS
  against WCRF and Harvard; NASEM against ICCPUD; CIM against EBM; the 2026 DGA's supporters and the
  ASN; vegan and omnivore readers. Keys that grade a contested question as contested: 7, 11, 16, 21,
  28. None keys a contested question as settled in either direction.

**Safety**
- **S1 (medium), project "Choosing the claim".** Lesson 11 as fixed tells a reader whose candidate
  claims are all about eating less or weight, and for whom food rules have grown, to take that to the
  callout first. The project, which is where that reader goes next, didn't say it.
- Checked and passing: the callout is byte-identical to lesson 1's in both files. Items 6, 8 and 9
  (weight) are about a columnist, an invented trial and a headline, and no key treats weight as a
  goal. Item 4 is a third party's questionnaire total, and its lesson is that the arithmetic can't be
  done on one person. Items 24 and 29 are the only ones touching medicines, and both keys send the
  question to a practitioner; neither distractor tells anyone to change a medicine. The project asks
  for no weight, no food log, and no health disclosure (step 6), and scores "ask first" in full.

**Project**
- **J1 (medium), worked model.** "A search for 'carnivore diet' and '2029' (the friend's 'two
  thousand' suggested a number)": nobody could search for 2029 from "two thousand". A step that
  can't be done, in the model the reader copies.
- **J2 (medium), rubric, Tracing.** The 3 descriptor allowed "marked untraceable" and then required
  the key sentence quoted and the design named, which an untraceable claim can't have, against the
  rubric's own promise that "Untraceable" scores as well as a tidy answer.
- **J3 (low), rubric, Guidance.** "Two guides found and quoted": step 5 says a guide saying nothing is
  a result, and silence can't be quoted.
- **J4 (low), step 3.** It went straight to established, contested or value and skipped lesson 11's
  bins (measurable, unmeasurable as stated, false), which lesson 11 puts first.
- **J5 (low), step 5.** No links to the five guides.
- **J6 (low), "Choosing the claim".** Its example of a good claim, "Eggs raise your cholesterol", is
  lesson 1's exercise claim and a lesson 6 case, beside a rule against the course's worked cases;
  and the list of worked cases left out eight glasses of water, lesson 11's, which many readers
  will have acted on.
- **J7 (low), rubric, Classification.** "A contested question called settled (in either direction)
  with no source" left unclear whether a source excused it.
- Checked and passing: seven steps and five 0-to-3 criteria with a pass at 10 of 15, as the outline
  specifies; doable with this course and a search engine; the decisions and "ask first" match lesson
  11; `minutes: 240` matches its own breakdown (half an hour, two hours, an hour and a half) and
  lesson 11's 20-minute first step; every quotation in the worked model matches the Lennerz abstract
  in `SOURCES.md` 7.5.
