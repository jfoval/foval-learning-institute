## Standing, for every lesson in this course

- **Sensitive domain, standards 3.4.** Neutrality pass on every lesson, audit before publish.
- **`SOURCES.md` has carried the lesson's errors, and in one direction.** At lesson 2's review, four
  findings were already in the research file (R3.3a, R3.3b, R3.3c, R3.3e, and the R2.10 hedges): a
  figure trimmed so it read opposite to the abstract, a criticism credited to a paper that doesn't
  make it, and a full-text paper recorded only for its critical half, leaving out the authors'
  own practical case for 220 minus age. All are corrected, with dated notes. **When a lesson rests
  on an entry, open the primary in `scratchpad/research/raw/` before trusting the summary**
  (drafting defect 26).
- **Martin 2025's limits are about ±20 beats for each formula.** The "±18–24" spans all seven
  formulas. Don't put ±24 around Tanaka (see R3.3c's 2026-09-24 note). And Martin's authors defend
  220 minus age as a practical starting point, so a lesson that uses Martin against the formula
  should also carry that.
- **Persinger's talk-test figures:** the 84 and 74 per cent heart-rate reserve are at the
  gas-measured threshold. At the talk test's own stage the figures were 80 and 81. Nothing read
  shows the talk test beating a heart-rate target.
- **Halperin's moderators are hedged**: "slightly" for near failure and for 12 reps or fewer,
  "trivially" for later sets. The heterogeneity is between studies. Between people it was
  "minimal" (SD 1.45 reps).
- **Terms lesson 2 now defines**, which later lessons can use without redefining: absolute and
  relative intensity, MET, ventilatory threshold, heart-rate reserve, limits of agreement, RPE, RIR.
- **Safety lines a competent instructor would give, and lesson 2's reviewer found missing:** any
  lesson that teaches heart-rate targets or zones carries the beta-blocker caution (Martin, R3.3c)
  or points back to lesson 2. Any exercise that takes a set to or near failure carries a
  keep-breathing line (R4.10) and a stable set-up (a chair without wheels, back to a wall).
- **"Per cent" in bodies and quizzes alike** (OUTLINE decision 8). Quoted "percent" stays as
  written.
- **Pipeline words stay out of learner text**: say "this course read the abstract only", not "the
  research file" or "the researcher".
- **Montero and Lundby 2017 is full text now, and it is 78 untrained young men, 18 to 35, in
  self-chosen groups with no untrained comparison group** (SOURCES R3.6b, corrected at lesson 3's
  review). Its abstract says "healthy adults". Any lesson citing it (lesson 4's non-responder
  passage, lesson 9 on training across a life) should say "young men", and shouldn't use it for
  women, older adults or patients: the authors' own limitation says so.
- **The statisticians' comparator test cuts both ways.** Atkinson and Batterham's point applies to
  the dose studies' individual counts as much as to HERITAGE. A lesson that uses it against one side
  should say so of the other. No reply from the HERITAGE investigators has been read.
- **Weston 2014's authors call HIIT "well tolerated and safe"** on limited data, with a screening
  and supervision condition and a concern about all-out sprints in clinical populations (R3.4b, now
  quoted). Report their reading beside the data; don't repeat "safe" as established.
- **Storoschuk 2025 declares that Gibala advises and holds equity in a company whose services partly
  relate to exercise** (R3.7c). State it once where the review is used, without insinuation.
- **Lesson 3 teaches lactate briefly** (Brooks, R3.10b: not a waste product; the lactate threshold
  as a blood-sample point paired with the ventilatory threshold) and leaves soreness and the acidosis
  dispute to lesson 8.
- **ACSM 2026 (R2.2) declares one interest, the Tonal advisory-board tie.** The Currier and
  Phillips authorship overlap with R2.3 is a fact to state, once, not a "declared interest". Lesson 1
  now says it that way; lesson 4 refers back to lesson 1 for both. *(Lesson 1's review F19, with
  lesson 4's F8.)*
- **Generation 100 facts now in R1.9, for lesson 3's HIIT arm and lesson 10:** the primary HR 0.92
  (0.58 to 1.47); HIIT against MICT unadjusted 0.49 (0.25 to 0.99), adjusted 0.51 (0.25 to 1.02);
  the training arms swapped two of five weekly guideline sessions, with supervision offered, not
  required; and the exclusion list, so its "no cardiovascular events" applies to screened
  volunteers. Karvinen's pleiotropy case rests on rats and twins (R1.10, corrected), and the MR
  study tested for pleiotropy (R1.11). *(Lesson 1's review, F2, F3, F4, F17, F24.)*

## From lesson 6's review (2026-09-24)

- **MacDougall 1985: the breath was only "a portion" of the rise** (R4.10, added from the abstract).
  Pressure also rose in the lifting half and fell in the lowering half. Lesson 2's "holding it under
  effort drives blood pressure up sharply" should be read against it: holding the breath adds to a
  rise that lifting to failure produces anyway. Any lesson that uses the study for "don't hold your
  breath" says so.
- **Callaghan 2001: compression made the injuries "more frequent and more severe", and the specimens
  were pig neck segments (C3-C4)** (R4.9, added). Lessons 9 (back pain) and 10 carry both if they
  mention the lumbar dispute.
- **AHA 2023: "Machines are likely safer than free weights for these patients"**, scoped to people
  with musculoskeletal limitations, advanced arthritis, severe osteoporosis, neuropathies or a
  previous stroke (R4.21, R4.26). It travels with any "machines are safer" claim in lessons 7, 9
  and 10; lesson 7's "The gym column isn't better than the home column" is about strength and
  needs no change.
- **AHA 2023's extended cool-down for people on blood-pressure medicine** (R4.10) belongs in lesson
  9's hypertension section and lesson 8's cool-down material.
- **Stop-for-pain line.** Lesson 6 now carries the NSCA Takeaway's pain-and-referral sentences
  (R5.31) and a labelled stop rule. Any lesson that teaches or prescribes a movement (7, 8, 9)
  should carry a stop-for-pain line. The NHS how-to page has one verbatim, "Stop the exercise
  immediately if you feel any pain or become unwell" (checked in `raw/r5/nhs_strength-and-flex-
  exercise-plan-how-to-videos.txt`), but R5.28 records only "a standard safety note": add the
  sentence to R5.28 before a lesson quotes it.
- **Open: the NHS Strength and Flex series has "pull-up" and "wide pull-up" videos that nobody has
  watched** (Brightcove on nhs.uk). If they use household furniture, they contradict lesson 6's
  warning about pulling from furniture and lesson 7's "a table that can't tip". Watch them and record
  what they are in R5.28 before any lesson recommends them; lesson 6's Go deeper now says they
  weren't watched.
- **Home pulls:** lesson 6 now says every NSCA pulling progression starts with equipment and points
  to lesson 7's labelled substitutes (one-arm backpack row, band row). Lesson 7 should keep those
  labels, and its "keep lesson 6's shoulder-blade cue" still works: lesson 6 now says the cue reads
  most naturally facing the floor, which a braced one-arm row does.
- **Quiz keys:** lesson 6 is now 3,2,0,1,2,0. Lesson 3 (2,0,3,1,3,0) and lesson 2 (1,3,0,2,0,3)
  shared first-four runs with suggested sequences; the validator compares only neighbours, so check
  the whole course's keys when reordering.
- **OUTLINE's "~75 min" for lesson 6 is short**: it measures 110, mostly six mandated videos
  (about 16 minutes, unpriced by the tool) and two 10-minute exercises. Record the exception in
  OUTLINE's "Lesson length" paragraph.
