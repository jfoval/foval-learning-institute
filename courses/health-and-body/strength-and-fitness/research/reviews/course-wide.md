## Standing, for every lesson in this course

- **Sensitive domain, standards 3.4.** Neutrality pass on every lesson, audit before publish.
- **`SOURCES.md` has carried the lesson's errors, and in one direction.** At lesson 2's review, four
  findings were already in the research file (R3.3a, R3.3b, R3.3c, R3.3e, and the R2.10 hedges): a
  figure trimmed so it read opposite to the abstract, a criticism credited to a paper that doesn't
  make it, and a full-text paper recorded only for its critical half, leaving out the authors'
  own practical case for 220 minus age. All are corrected, with dated notes. **When a lesson rests
  on an entry, open the primary in `scratchpad/research/raw/` before trusting the summary**
  (drafting defect 26).
- **Martin 2025's limits are about ±20 beats for each formula.** The "±18–24" spans all seven
  formulas. Don't put ±24 around Tanaka (see R3.3c's 2026-09-24 note). And Martin's authors defend
  220 minus age as a practical starting point, so a lesson that uses Martin against the formula
  should also carry that.
- **Persinger's talk-test figures:** the 84 and 74 per cent heart-rate reserve are at the
  gas-measured threshold. At the talk test's own stage the figures were 80 and 81. Nothing read
  shows the talk test beating a heart-rate target.
- **Halperin's moderators are hedged**: "slightly" for near failure and for 12 reps or fewer,
  "trivially" for later sets. The heterogeneity is between studies. Between people it was
  "minimal" (SD 1.45 reps).
- **Terms lesson 2 now defines**, which later lessons can use without redefining: absolute and
  relative intensity, MET, ventilatory threshold, heart-rate reserve, limits of agreement, RPE, RIR.
- **Safety lines a competent instructor would give, and lesson 2's reviewer found missing:** any
  lesson that teaches heart-rate targets or zones carries the beta-blocker caution (Martin, R3.3c)
  or points back to lesson 2. Any exercise that takes a set to or near failure carries a
  keep-breathing line (R4.10) and a stable set-up (a chair without wheels, back to a wall).
- **"Per cent" in bodies and quizzes alike** (OUTLINE decision 8). Quoted "percent" stays as
  written.
- **Pipeline words stay out of learner text**: say "this course read the abstract only", not "the
  research file" or "the researcher".
- **Montero and Lundby 2017 is full text now, and it is 78 untrained young men, 18 to 35, in
  self-chosen groups with no untrained comparison group** (SOURCES R3.6b, corrected at lesson 3's
  review). Its abstract says "healthy adults". Any lesson citing it (lesson 4's non-responder
  passage, lesson 9 on training across a life) should say "young men", and shouldn't use it for
  women, older adults or patients: the authors' own limitation says so.
- **The statisticians' comparator test cuts both ways.** Atkinson and Batterham's point applies to
  the dose studies' individual counts as much as to HERITAGE. A lesson that uses it against one side
  should say so of the other. No reply from the HERITAGE investigators has been read.
- **Weston 2014's authors call HIIT "well tolerated and safe"** on limited data, with a screening
  and supervision condition and a concern about all-out sprints in clinical populations (R3.4b, now
  quoted). Report their reading beside the data; don't repeat "safe" as established.
- **Storoschuk 2025 declares that Gibala advises and holds equity in a company whose services partly
  relate to exercise** (R3.7c). State it once where the review is used, without insinuation.
- **Lesson 3 teaches lactate briefly** (Brooks, R3.10b: not a waste product; the lactate threshold
  as a blood-sample point paired with the ventilatory threshold) and leaves soreness and the acidosis
  dispute to lesson 8.
