# Reviews: Sleep lesson 2

## 2026-09-19 — Lesson 02 — Stage 4, all passes, Tier A
**Verdicts as returned:** depth pass, fact-check pass with five defects, **neutrality pass on the
risk it was most exposed to**, pedagogy pass on quiz mechanics and fail on one check, cold start pass
with two gaps, voice and media pass. Twenty findings, none critical.

**The lesson's central discipline held.** The abstract names no task and the lesson names none
anywhere: not in the body, the quiz, either exercise, Connections or Go deeper. It says so four times
and refuses the temptation explicitly. The reviewer called this the single most likely place the
course could have fabricated.

### Findings

1. **[major] The study's doses are hours in bed and the lesson read them as hours slept**, in the
   worked example, the checkpoint and three quiz options. Six hours asleep is more sleep than the
   six-hour arm got, so the reading made the implication about a real reader stronger than the
   evidence. Defect 19 in its purest form, in the one place the lesson is trying hardest to be
   careful. **Applied:** the unit is stated once where the dose is introduced, and every later "six
   hours" reads "six hours in bed".
2. **[major] "Delta power is the amount of slow electrical activity in the sleep trace" was
   unsourced**, and the next clause said the course had read nothing that could have supplied it.
   Defect 22's third form, refuting itself two clauses later. **Applied:** labelled as background the
   course is supplying, in the body and in the Sources block.
3. **[major] "You would have concluded after three nights" put a number on the plateau that the
   abstract does not support.** **Applied:** "early in the fortnight", with no number.
4. **[major] The lesson called its own exercise section 2 of the project, which the outline defines
   as something else, and pointed at an empty folder.** Defect 12, the second lesson to do it.
   **Applied:** the exercise is now preparation for the log section, and the project exists.
5. **[major] "An all-nighter" is one night; the arm was three**, and "put the other way round"
   asserted that the Conclusions' equivalence restates the Results' disproportionality finding, which
   it does not. **Applied:** both separated.
6. **[medium] A non-significant difference reported as an equality, three times.** The source says
   the ratings "did not significantly differentiate" the two conditions; the lesson said the groups
   rated themselves about the same. This course exists to police exactly that move, and the quiz
   explanation had it right. **Applied**, using the quiz's own wording.
7. **[medium] A between-subjects design described as within-subjects.** Participants were randomised
   to one dose, so the six-hour people were never in the four-hour condition. **Applied.**
8. **[medium] The section heading said two nights and the section said three.** **Applied.**
9. **[medium] A quiz stem attributed normalised wording to the abstract**, silently fixing the
   journal's "nearlinearly" under an attribution verb, where the research file gives a standing
   instruction about that word. **Applied:** the stem no longer attributes wording.
10. **[medium] Quiz item 2 re-ran the lesson's own worked example with the name changed.** Defect 5b,
    and discounting it left the lesson at two application items of six. **Applied:** recast onto
    somebody who lost three nights and then slept long for a week and asks whether they have caught
    up, which tests cumulative against acute.
11. **[medium] The hardest idea in the lesson had no checkpoint.** The fitted-parameter distinction
    gets a subsection and is never checked before the quiz. **Applied:** a checkpoint asking what is
    wrong with treating 15.84 hours as a safe limit, which also catches that "safely" is not in the
    sentence the paper wrote.
12. **[medium] Contractions at 4.8 per 1,000, under the band**, concentrated in the opening. The cut
    from 18.0 overshot downward. **Applied:** four restorations rather than a sweep.
13. **[medium] The second exercise asks the reader to come back in a fortnight and the stated minutes
    could not see it.** **Applied:** the cue rewritten so the model counts it, and the delayed sitting
    named as outside the lesson's time.
14. **[low] "Suggest" capitalised inside its own quotation marks**, in a lesson arguing that
    alterations inside quotation marks matter. **Applied.**
15. **[low] "The sentence the paper is famous for" is a claim about reception with nothing behind
    it.** **Applied.**
16. **[low] "The deficits were still growing at the end of the fortnight" is past what "cumulative"
    supports.** **Applied:** the abstract reports no point at which they stopped.
17. **[low] "Nobody was allowed to nap" narrowed a quotation that covers any sleep outside the
    assigned window**, which is what makes the dose a dose. **Applied.**
18. **[low] The Nadia example invites the reader to do the work and then does it for them.**
    **Not applied**, on the reviewer's own reading that the linter would not call it a self-check and
    that the second predict block asks a different question; the worked example with a gap is the
    predict that follows it.
19. **[low] Five one-line closing morals, a shape lesson 1 also has.** **Applied:** two cut.
20. **[low] Two quiz items test material no objective names.** **Applied:** objective 1 widened to
    name which of the study's numbers came out of a model, which makes the lesson's best idea
    examinable on the record.
