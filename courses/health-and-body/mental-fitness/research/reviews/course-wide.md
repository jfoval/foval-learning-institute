### What single-lesson reviews could not see: the counts, across nine lessons

**Architecture, psychological against pharmacological.** About forty sources are on psychological,
self-help or lifestyle methods and five are drug-specific (Kirsch, Cipriani, Stone, Moncrieff,
Jauhar). That is the course's declared scope (`course.yaml`, lesson 1's leave-out list, outline
decision 3), not a tilt, and it is how the drug side is treated where it appears that matters. Lesson
2's "trap" paragraph and lesson 8's "What the placebo group got" both warn, in the course's voice,
against the one comparison that would flatter therapy over drugs (0.79 against waiting lists beside
0.3 against a pill). Head-to-head results appear in lessons 2, 5 and 6; every one in the file is
"no significant difference" or favours the non-drug method, and the file holds no drug-favourable
head-to-head that was left out (Cuijpers 2023's combined-treatment sentence favours CBT further).
**The one architectural gap was lesson 4** (N1): it described NICE's step 3 for GAD as psychological
only and left out 1.2.17, NICE's own statement that neither mode is better.

**Lesson 8, per side.** Critique (Kirsch, Moncrieff) and defence (Cipriani, Jauhar): about twenty
distinct quotations each by hand count; source markers 27 against 26; Stone, the bridge, 19. Each
side has at least one of its own concessions quoted (Kirsch's "easily attained statistical
significance", Moncrieff's AMSTAR-2 grading and family-history clause; Cipriani's "long-lasting
debate" and risk-of-bias figures, Jauhar's "Admittedly"). Interests reported for both serotonin
papers and for Stone, and the gap for Kirsch 2008 and Cipriani stated. **Explained, not only
quoted:** Kirsch's threshold and severity reasoning; Cipriani's dropout and precision arguments;
Moncrieff's reason it matters; Jauhar's evidence. **The one side not explained** is the defenders'
case for why a modest consistent effect matters clinically, and the lesson says in terms that the
course has read no such statement and will not write one (Stage 4 N2, D2). That is disclosed, not
hidden, so it passes; it is still a research gap (see "Not fixed").

**Misconception lists, whole course.** 38 entries. On the method axis, 12 correct an enthusiast's
overreach and 7 a sceptic's (mirrors in lessons 3, 5, 6 and 7: "face-to-face is always better",
"it's all placebo", "mindfulness is all hype", "BA is just keeping busy", "positivity was debunked",
"the ratio paper was retracted", "the mindset research found nothing"). On the drug axis, 3 correct a
critic-direction error ("no better than placebo", "if the serotonin theory is wrong, the drugs don't
work", "0.3 means everyone gets a small benefit") and 2 a defender-direction one ("fix a chemical
imbalance", "the improvement is all the drug"). The rest are about the reader's own case. Balanced.

**Harms, whole course.** Drug harms in three lessons (5: sertraline's adverse events; 6: Hoge's 8%
dropout and 78.6% against 15.4%, Goyal's "without the associated toxicities"; 8: Stone's
"anorgasmia"). Non-drug harms in three (3: harm "lacking" and "rarely reported"; 5: injuries; 6: NIH
on meditation, MBSR's exclusions, NICE's mindfulness caution, MBSR's own 15.4%). Balanced, **except
stopping**: "most people stop antidepressants successfully" appeared in lessons 5, 6, 8 and 9, and
NG222's own withdrawal caveat nowhere (N3).

**Last words.** Lesson 5 ends its reviews on the course's "none of them contradicts the Cochrane
head-to-head finding"; lesson 6's "comparable" section on "This course does not choose between
them"; lesson 7's ratio on the course's summary, now followed by a scope line (N6); lesson 8's number
on the threshold as a judgement with Kirsch's severity finding standing either way, Stone on
"weighting should be done at the individual level", serotonin on the course's classification. No
side gets the last word in more than one set piece.

### Perspectives checked, and what each would object to

1. **CBT therapist, NICE-aligned clinician.** Would accept lessons 1 to 4 as fair to CBT: the
   waiting-list inflation is applied to CBT's own 0.79, and Mayo-Wilson's "probably clinically
   superior" is kept whole. Would object to lesson 4 treating step 3 as a psychological menu (N1).
2. **Critical-psychiatry reader (Kirsch, Moncrieff sympathetic).** Would object to: the drug-placebo
   gap called "the part that came from the drug" as if the trials had settled that it was pharmacological
   (N2); lesson 8 making the subgroup argument against Kirsch's threshold in one direction only (N4);
   lesson 9's Stone row licensing only the subgroup reading (N5); and above all four lessons repeating
   "most people stop antidepressants successfully" with no mention of NICE's withdrawal caveat (N3).
   Would accept Moncrieff's case as fairly stated, with its concessions and its reason for mattering.
   The omitted Moncrieff finding on long-term use and serotonin was judged at Stage 4 and not reopened.
3. **Prescribing psychiatrist.** Would object to lesson 4 (N1), and would note that drug harms appear
   incidentally in lessons 5 and 6 from non-drug trials while drug benefit appears only as a disputed
   0.3. That second point does not survive the count above: harms run both ways, and lesson 2 defends
   the drugs' 0.3 against the unfair comparison. Would want a primary defender statement on clinical
   significance (not fixable here).
4. **A patient who takes an antidepressant.** Lesson 8's opening and closing callout, "Two claims,
   not one" and the "no verdict" framing all hold. Would object to nothing that a sentence could fix
   beyond N2 and N3, which cut both ways for this reader: N3 adds a caution, N2 adds that the trials
   may understate as well as overstate.
5. **Mindfulness teacher.** Lesson 6 gives the teacher a named voice on scope, keeps Galante's "high"
   confidence and Kuyken, and reports Hoge. Would object to the opening "each has been sold further
   than its evidence goes", said of all three methods in the course's voice with no source, and of BA,
   which the same lesson calls the method most people haven't heard of (N9), and to the Kuyken
   checkpoint's "fairest" (N7).
6. **Positive-psychology researcher.** Lesson 7 is careful (the critics' scope, "does at least provide
   some empirical evidence", Fredrickson's weaker claim, the correction's "remain valid"). Would object
   that positive psychology appears in the course only as a failed claim, which could read as a verdict
   on the field (N6).
7. **A person of faith who uses prayer or religious practice for stress.** Nothing in the course
   mocks or asserts a religious claim; no lesson mentions religion, prayer or meditation's religious
   roots. The silence is not improper in itself, but lesson 1's list of what the course leaves out is
   explicit about everything else, so a practice many readers use was the one thing neither covered nor
   named (N10).
8. **Someone struggling who reads it.** Every lesson passes: "not a sign that self-help has failed"
   (1, 9), "not being helped isn't a test of severity" (3), "says nothing about anyone's character" (7),
   no method promised to work for the reader (2, 9). Lesson 3's "if your symptoms are more than mild"
   asks the reader to judge severity, which lesson 9 says needs an assessment; it points toward help,
   not away from it, so it was left, and is noted here.

### Findings

- **N1 (lesson 4, "What NICE offers").** Step 3 was given as "if a person chooses a high-intensity
  psychological treatment", with CG113 1.2.17 left out: "Base the choice of treatment on the person's
  preference as there is no evidence that either mode of treatment (individual high-intensity
  psychological intervention or drug treatment) is better." Selection (defect 14), against the drug
  side, in the lesson that teaches what NICE offers for GAD. `SOURCES.md` §1 permits saying drug
  treatment exists at step 3 and that the choice is the person's with a clinician.
- **N2 (lesson 8, "What the placebo group got", and quiz 2's explanation).** "So the 0.3 is the part
  that came from the drug" asserts in the course's voice that the drug-placebo gap is the drug's
  effect, which the critics contest (functional unblinding, reported later in the same lesson) and
  the defenders say may be an underestimate (Cipriani's dropout argument). A defender's reading
  stated as settled (defect 6).
- **N3 (lessons 5, 6, 8, 9, closing callouts).** NG222 1.4.12's "most people stop antidepressants
  successfully" appeared four times and 1.4.14's "withdrawal can sometimes be more difficult, with
  symptoms lasting longer (in some cases several weeks, and occasionally several months)" never,
  although `SOURCES.md` §2 records it from a full read. The Stage 1 rule allowing only 1.4.12 was
  written to keep tapering method (1.4.16) out, not to keep the caveat out, and the caveat is advice
  in neither direction. Omitted fact a well-informed critic would insist on.
- **N4 (lesson 8, the paragraph after the invented example).** "An average of 1.8 can come from a
  world in which some people are well past 3 points" makes the subgroup argument against Kirsch's
  threshold in one direction only; the lesson's own world one is the other direction.
- **N5 (lesson 9, table, Stone row).** The licence column gave only the defender's reading of Stone
  ("may hide a group who benefit a lot"); lesson 8 says the paper is a bridge that both sides can
  use, and the critic's half (most improvement in the same middle band on drug and placebo) was
  missing. Lesson 9's misconception 1 also led into Stone's model-based conclusion with "found"
  (defect 19).
- **N6 (lesson 7, end of the ratio section).** Positive psychology appears only through the ratio;
  no sentence said the case is not a verdict on the field, and `SOURCES.md` §14 asks lessons to avoid
  reading "as a debunking of positive psychology".
- **N7 (lesson 6, Kuyken checkpoint).** "The fairest narrow case for mindfulness against a drug in
  this course's file" contradicts the Hoge trial later in the same lesson. Consistency.
- **N8 (lesson 2, opening).** "The next six lessons ... and the one after them is about medication"
  miscounts: lessons 3 to 7 are five, and lesson 8 is medication. Consistency, one word.
- **N9 (lesson 6, opening).** "Each has been sold further than its evidence goes": an unsourced,
  course-voice charge against all three methods, including BA, which the lesson says most people
  haven't heard of. Loaded framing (3.3).
- **N10 (lesson 1, "What this course leaves out").** Religious and spiritual practice was neither
  covered nor named as left out.
