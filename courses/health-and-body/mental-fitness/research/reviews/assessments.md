# Reviews: Mental Fitness assessments

No course had an assessments review file before this one, so this follows the lesson reviews' shape.
Files: `assessments/final-test.md` (18 items) and `assessments/project-the-programme-you-were-offered.md`.

## 2026-09-24 — Final test and project — Stage 4, one reviewer

**Verdicts.** **Facts pass**: every key was right by what the lessons teach and every quotation in
the test string-matches a lesson or `SOURCES.md`. One figure was ambiguous (F5) and one key reached
past its lesson (F7). **Pedagogy fail, now fixed**: the test could be passed on shape (P1), one item
copied a lesson quiz item (P2), and only 11 of 18 items applied the course to new material (P3).
**Safety pass with one fix**: no item asks readers to classify their own symptoms, no item gives
medication advice, and the project is not a mood log, but the test page had no crisis callout (S1).
**Project pass with fixes** (J1 to J4). **Voice pass**: no em dashes, no spaced en dashes, no banned
words, but the project's contractions were below the band (V1).

Allocation matched the outline (2, 3, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 1), and all three cross-lesson items were
present (items 6, 10, 17).

### Findings

**Pedagogy**
- **P1 (high), the whole test.** The key was spottable by shape. The mechanical heuristics were
  clear before the review: the key was the sole longest option in 3 of 18, the sole shortest in 3,
  the only option without an absolute in 2, the only hedged option in 2, and the only option without
  a reason clause in 1. **By judgement, "pick the calibrated middle option" scored about 14 of 18
  (78%).** The distractors were extremes ("None", "Nothing", "backwards", "worthless", "can't show
  anything") and the key was the one moderate answer in items 1, 2, 4 to 8, 11 to 15, 17 and 18. That
  is the same failure this course's lessons had.
- **P2 (high), item 18.** It was a near copy of lesson 9's quiz item 1: a journal, a trial, "so" plus
  advice, "measurable as a whole since a trial was cited", and "unmeasurable since it's a feeling".
- **P3 (medium).** Items 2, 7, 8, 11, 15 and 16 asked what a source said, and 18 was a copy, so only
  11 of 18 applied the course to material in no lesson. The outline asks for at least 13.
- **P4 (medium), item 2.** It was recall ("Why does CG113 put education... at step 1"). Its key's
  quotation was the one lesson 9's quiz item 3 grades, and it claimed "in the guideline's words"
  for a paraphrase ("milder").
- **P5 (medium), items 1, 5, 6, 12 and 17.** Stems that assumed a flaw ("What's wrong", "What's the
  problem", "What's the flaw") sat above "None" or "Nothing" options, which the stem rules out.
- **P6 (low), noted and not changed.** Item 3 uses lesson 2's conversion table (0.2 to 58 percent),
  which can't be avoided. Item 13 follows the structure of lesson 6's checkpoint on a lower bound past
  the margin, with new numbers. Item 15 shares a theme with lesson 7's quiz item 1 (training and
  "years") but tests a different outcome, anxiety.

**Facts**
- **F5 (medium), item 10.** The stem quoted "the Cochrane review's 0.67" for exercise, unsigned, in a
  test whose item 6 uses a different Cochrane review's 0.67 (self-help for anxiety, positive). The
  lesson prints the exercise figure as -0.67.
- **F7 (medium), item 8.** The key read "NICE doesn't name single worry techniques". Lesson 4's
  finding is narrower: no GAD recommendation names one.
- **F8 (low), item 17.** "The third shape in lesson 7's wider sense" came straight after a reference
  to Sleep lesson 7, but the widening is this course's lesson 7.

**Safety**
- **S1 (medium), test body.** It pointed readers to "the box at the top of every lesson", and the test
  page has no box. It also said "Two questions are about research on antidepressants", which doesn't
  match the items: 12 and 16 are about antidepressant research, and 17 is about an invented panic drug.

**Project**
- **J1 (medium), Part 3.** It said "not stated" had been met in an abstract by lessons 2 and 4.
  Lesson 2 doesn't meet it; lessons 4 and 7 do. It also added a placebo pill and "nobody assigned" to
  the end of a list introduced as running from easiest to hardest, which ranks them where no lesson
  did.
- **J2 (medium), Part 4.** "Go to the recommendations" gave no link to the chapter or way to find the
  numbers. The specification to use for a group course was never given, although 1.2.15 was named.
- **J3 (medium), Part 2.** "Traced" was used, and scored in the rubric, without being defined.
- **J4 (low).** "Most programmes make between three and ten claims" is an unsourced empirical claim.
  "Showed how often the two differ" claims a frequency that no lesson measured.

**Voice**
- **V1 (low), project.** 8 contractions in 1,771 body words, 4.5 per 1,000 against the 5 to 8 band.

The project otherwise meets the outline: four parts, a closing paragraph, a five-row rubric with
two honesty rows, no medicines, no mood log, the crisis callout verbatim, a stop-and-seek-help
paragraph, and about three hours of work with the ten-claim cap.

### Resolutions applied (2026-09-24)

- **P1.** Every item now has at least one hedged or balanced wrong option ("probably", "roughly",
  "partly", "may", "both about equally"), and several keys are the plain, assertive option. Measured
  after the fix: the key is the sole longest option in 2 of 18, the sole shortest in 4, the only
  option without an absolute in 1, the only hedged option in 0, the most hedged in 0, the only one
  with a reason clause in 0, and the only one without in 2. By judgement, "calibrated middle" now
  scores about 2 of 18 (items 6 and 13). Option-length spread is at most 14 characters. The keys went
  from BDACADBCDACBADCBDA to **CADABCDBACBDCDABDA**, with A 5, B 4, C 4, D 5, no adjacent repeat and
  no regular cycle.
- **P2.** Item 18 is new. It gives an association welded to a cause by "which means", and it tests
  "not shown" against "false" alongside the sort.
- **P3, P4.** Item 2 is new and applied: a podcast calling itself "step 1 care". Item 7 is now
  applied: two invented users with PHQ-9 scores of 7 and 16 against Karyotaki's severity finding.
  They are third parties with given scores, so decision 2 holds. That makes 14 applied items (1 to 7,
  9, 10, 12 to 14, 17, 18). Items 8, 11, 15 and 16 still ask what a source found, as the test's
  introduction says a few do.
- **P5.** The stems are neutral now ("How does that sentence stand", "How does the advert stand",
  "How does that comparison stand", "How do those trials bear on the offer"), and the "None" options
  are replaced.
- **F5.** The stem now names "the 2026 Cochrane review", and the explanation says Cochrane printed
  -0.67 and that lesson 3's review printed its improvement as a positive 0.67.
- **F7.** The key now reads "no GAD recommendation names postponement or any single worry technique".
- **F8.** The explanation now reads "the third shape as this course's lesson 7 widened it".
- **S1.** The verbatim crisis callout is added after the test's first paragraph, and the closing
  paragraph points to it. "Two questions" became "A few questions touch on research into medicines".
- **J1.** Lesson 2's five are listed in order, then "Add the two the course met later" (a placebo pill,
  lesson 8; nobody assigned, lesson 7's survey). "Not stated" now cites lessons 4 and 7.
- **J2.** There's a direct link to CG113's Recommendations chapter, an instruction to search the page
  for "1.2.12", and a rule for choosing: 1.2.13 alone, 1.2.14 with a person, 1.2.15 for a class.
- **J3.** "Traced" is defined in Part 2: you've opened what the figure cites and found the number
  there, or found that it isn't there.
- **J4.** The claim count is replaced by the cap alone, and "how often" became "how the two can differ".
- **V1.** Contractions are now 7.6 per 1,000.
- **Checks.** Every explanation was re-read against the option its key now selects, and distractors
