### Three errors in the lesson's own teaching

**L6-01 the opening's load-bearing sentence was false.** "Nothing about the second task was harder
for a person." The outside-frontier task was built out of the sort of business cases BCG uses in its
own hiring, and the control group still only got it right 84.5% of the time. It was hard. **Closed,
and the correct version is better than the sentence it replaces**: it was chosen because it is the
kind of hard that people are comparatively good at, which is what the frontier means and what the
false sentence was reaching for.

**L6-02 the quotation was trimmed of the qualifier that complicates the lesson's own next section**,
which is defect 9 by name. The paper says the task was designed so AI would struggle "at least
without extensive guidance and human intervention", and the lesson stopped at "struggle". **The
dropped clause is the one that makes the overview result land**, because the overview group is the
group that had the guidance and it did worst anyway. Restored, and the next section now points at
it.

**L6-03 the predict block mis-sorted a case under the lesson's own taxonomy**, in the one place the
reader is told to check their own sorting. A donor list was filed under "something nobody has
written down" when a donor list is written down; it is a fact outside the request, which is the
first unreliable kind. The escape clause "anywhere the system can reach" quietly collapsed the third
kind into the first and took the scheme's edges with it. **Closed**, and the block is stronger for
it, because pasting the list in is now a clean demonstration of the general move it already claimed.

### Sourcing

**L6-05 the scope was missing from the body of the lesson most dependent on one study.** No BCG, no

**L6-07 an invented claim about people in the lesson's third sentence**, that most people asked in

**L6-08 the label did not travel into the quiz.** The body says honestly that the four-part scheme is
the course's reasoning rather than a finding, and then five of six items graded the reader on it
with no such note anywhere. This is the third lesson where a labelled inference reached the
assessment unlabelled. **Closed:** two explanations now say it.

**L6-09 a capability asserted as standing fact.** "Nothing in the process goes and looks" is true of
a plain chat window and false of products with a search tool, which the course knows because lesson
7 measures it. A reader on such a product would find the sorting rule mis-sorting on their first
try. **Closed** with a callout that says what the tool does and does not change, and adds one
question to the fact cases: did it go and look, and can I see what it found?
