# Reviews: Using AI Effectively lesson 10, "What you hand over"

## 2026-09-18 — Lesson 10 — facts, quotations, sourcing, decisions, pedagogy, quiz, voice, Tier A

**STATUS: REVIEWED AND APPLIED, same session.** Came back **FAIL**, in the same shape lesson 6
failed: everything a script checks was green, and it failed on content inside its own teaching
devices. The reviewer was briefed to be hard on jurisdiction and on datedness, because this is the
lesson most likely to go stale and the one most likely to be read as legal advice.

### The finding that carried the verdict

**L10-01 the lesson stated current market behaviour as standing fact, in the lesson whose whole
thesis is that you must not.** Four places. "Sometimes. Often not, especially on organisational
plans, and frequently switchable." "It is rarely the same answer." And an employer's arrangement
being "usually" a different contract, three times over in one paragraph, plus "frequently" in the
worked case.

Decision 2's own test applies and every one of them fails it: a provider changing a default next
quarter makes the sentence false, and there is no model, no product, no date and no source.
Footnote 3 is honest that product behaviour cannot be sourced here, but that licenses the absence of
a citation, not the body asserting the shape of the market. **The harm route is the one the lesson
names sixteen lines earlier**: a reader in 2027 holds a belief about a document instead of reading
it, which is the failure this lesson exists to cure.

**Closed, by trimming rather than rewriting**, because each entry already ended on the correct
sentence. The employer's arrangement is now "a separate document with its own terms", which is true
whatever the terms turn out to be, and the training belief now answers with where the answer lives
rather than with what it probably says.

### Two cross-references that were false

a grep across its twelve lessons and both assessments for erasure, GDPR and the right to be

checked, which is L11-03 and L4-05 again, and it was the sentence carrying the lesson's own
jurisdiction promise, which makes it the worst one in the file to have invented. **Closed** by
making the claim about this course's convention, which is true and needs no other course to prop it
up.

**L10-03 a forward reference lesson 11 does not honour.** "The claims companies make about exactly
what this lesson asks you to check." Lesson 11 contains no claim about training, retention or data.
**Closed**, and pointed at the move lesson 11 does teach, which this lesson has just used.
