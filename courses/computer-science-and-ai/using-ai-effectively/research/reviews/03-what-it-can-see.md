### The five that mattered

**L3-01 three of nine quotations had been silently altered.** Two lowercased a sentence-initial
capital with no bracket and pulled a full stop inside the quotation marks; one set a word in bold
*inside* the quoted matter with no "emphasis mine", and a second did the same to a phrase from Liu
et al. Each is small. Together, as the reviewer put it, they say that the quotation discipline this
course is proudest of is being applied by eye. **Closed:** capitals restored in brackets, emphasis
moved outside the quotation marks in both places.

**L3-02 "a group at Stanford" for a seven-author paper spanning several institutions.** Three of the
seven are at Stanford and two are not. This is the third lesson running in which every quotation was
verbatim and the sentence with no quotation marks in it was wrong, which is why `course-wide.md`
tells a reviewer to point at those sentences first. **Closed:** the body now names Liu and six
colleagues, and the misconception list names them rather than "the Stanford result".

**L3-03 the lesson told the reader to go and look at figures nobody here has opened.** Go deeper
said to read the abstract "and look at the figures. The shape of the curve, high at both ends and
sagging in the middle, is worth seeing once." The Liu entry in SOURCES is **abstract read, paper not
opened**, so the curve is a description of something this course has not seen. **Closed:** the entry
now says plainly that the course read the abstract and not the paper, and points the reader at the
abstract for the result and the paper for the measurements.

**L3-04 the capability figure carried a date and no model, which decision 2 forbids**, and the
lesson printed two different figures from the same chapter two clauses apart with no
acknowledgement, which reads as a contradiction inside one sentence. **Closed, and the fix is better
than the finding.** The two figures now get a paragraph: the defining passage says thousands of
tokens, the chapter summary characterises transformer language models in general as hundreds of
thousands to millions, and the lesson says that **neither is a measurement of any product**. That is
what it is, and saying so is more useful than either number.

**L3-05 the labelled stretch did not travel into the quiz.** The body does the honest thing well,
naming what Liu et al. varied, saying conversations were not studied, and marking its own extension
as "the stretch, marked as mine". Then quiz item 2 grades the reader on that extension as though it
were the finding. **Closed:** the explanation now carries the label.

### The missing required element

### Sourcing and decisions

**L3-07 two product claims stated flatly with no source**, in a lesson whose own callout models the
right handling for a third. **Closed:** both now say what the callout says, which is that this
course has not surveyed the products and that the answer belongs to the reader's product rather than
to the technology.

**L3-08 a section number SOURCES could not check.** The lesson sends a reader to "section 7.6 on
decoding and sampling". Chapter 7 was read in full so it is right, and nothing in the research file

**L3-09 decision 5 breached by one exercise.** The product-page exercise had no prediction and no
comparison, in a course whose fifth decision is that every exercise's deliverable is a comparison.
**Closed:** it now asks the reader to write down what they think the answers are before looking, and
to mark which predictions were wrong. The reviewer's judgement that this is pedagogically the best
moment in the lesson is right: most readers are wrong about at least one.

**L3-10 one step assumed an interface.** "Can you turn either off, and is it on or off right now for
your account?" presupposes both an account and a toggle, which an employer-provisioned product may
not have. **Closed** with a clause.
