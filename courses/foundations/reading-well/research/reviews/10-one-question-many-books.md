## 2026-09-18 — Lesson 10 — depth, fact-check and pedagogy, Tier A
**Reviewer:** fresh-context subagent, which fetched Gutenberg #1293 and #575, the APS speed-reading
summary, the Brysbaert PDF and the Harvard page directly. **All seven Adler quotations verbatim
against `SOURCES.md`**, and then one non-Adler quotation that is verbatim from nothing.

**Findings:**

1. [critical] **A quotation attributed to Rayner and colleagues that neither permitted source
   contains.** The lesson had them saying rates above a thousand words a minute "are not possible
   without severe loss of comprehension". The APS summary contains no "severe" and states no rate.
   The nearest real text is **Brysbaert's** first sentence, and the lesson had altered it twice while
   keeping the quotation marks. Lesson 4, which owns the source, states the same ceiling without
   quotation marks and is right to.
2. [critical] **The worked example's central move was false about Ruskin, and the lecture refutes it
   twice.** The lesson said Ruskin "has no concept of a pass at all" and "never addresses the case of
   a difficulty met in the middle of a first reading". Four hundred words before the sentence the
   lesson quotes, Ruskin writes "whenever you are in doubt about a word, hunt it down patiently" and
   "never let a word escape you that looks suspicious"; elsewhere he says a book is not serviceable
   "until it has been read, and re-read". He has a pass and he answers the question, the opposite way.
   **The lesson made its own payoff come out tidily by asserting a silence the author does not have**,
   three lessons after teaching Adler's rule against disagreeing disputatiously.
3. [critical] **Step 5's output was put in Adler's mouth and `SOURCES.md` did not carry it**, in a
   section every paragraph of which is footnoted to him. It was load-bearing beyond one sentence:
   it was objective 1, quiz item 4's key, and item 4's dismissal of a distractor. Course-wide finding
   3, for the fifth time.
4. [high] "they reached it from a book, a paper and a library guide independently" is contradicted by
   this course's own research file, which says Harvard's habits map onto Adler.
5. [high] The Harvard translation imported a prohibition the page does not contain. Its habit is to
   hear yourself asking questions and write them down as "reminders of the unfinished business you
   still have with a text". There is no instruction to defer settling them, and that is exactly what
   the worked example needed Harvard to say.
6. [high] The Keshav translation inverted him. His second pass contains its own deferrals, "read with
   care but ignore proofs" and mark the references rather than chasing them, so the tidy ladder of
   defer-then-resolve is not his.
7. [high] **The lesson misreported lesson 1 and reversed its teaching**, twice, including in the
   course's last opening sentence. Lesson 1 says the levels are nested and that no level is better in
   itself. "It is the level the other nine were for" is the hierarchy-of-worth claim lesson 1 spent a
   section refusing.
8. [high] **Lesson 1 promises a closing exercise this lesson never delivered.** Lesson 1 tells the
   reader to keep a list because "the last exercise in the course asks you to run it again, and the
   change between the two is the only evidence that any of this worked". A reader who did as asked
   for nine lessons was left holding it.
9. [high] "The project brief has the rest" pointed at a file that did not exist.
10. [medium] Ruskin's em dash silently replaced with a comma and a footnote that mis-described the
    substitution, in a course whose rule is that quoted text keeps what its author wrote and whose
    build now permits it; "he is writing about clerks", invented; the Bacon close extended Bacon's
    *reason* as well as his reference, borrowing a four-hundred-year-old authority for an argument
    that is the lesson's own and better; lesson 1's caveat on the same Bacon passage dropped and then
    contradicted; an unsourced quantitative claim in Adler's voice; one think-block in a five-step
    spine, with the terms simply handed over; a conduct claim about sellers no source carries; and an
    exercise that asked for four book surveys inside a sitting lesson 2 budgets at one survey an hour.
11. [low] Bacon's *Essays* dated to 1625 without lesson 1's care; a grammar slip; lesson 4 unnamed in
    a Connections paragraph that claims all nine; and, course-wide, lesson 1 rendering the Bacon
    passage with its quotation mark one word late so that Bacon's "also" is silently dropped.

**Resolutions:** all applied, and three of them made the lesson better rather than merely correct.

**Finding 2's repair kept the structure and made it true.** Ruskin now answers, in his own words, and
the apparent three-to-one vote resolves into a sharper question rather than into nothing: whether the
prohibition is a property of the first pass or of reading as such. Two issues now survive step 4
where the draft had one, and neither is manufactured.

**Finding 3 was fixed at the root rather than hedged.** The scan was re-opened and Adler does have
the position, under a name, and his version is better than the drafter's: "The special quality that a
syntopical analysis tries to achieve can, indeed, be summarized in the two words 'dialectical
objectivity.' The syntopical reader, in short, tries to look at all sides and to take no sides." With
his own admission that it is an ideal nobody meets, and that the half you fail at is looking at all
sides. That is now in `SOURCES.md` and in the lesson, and quiz item 4 rests on it.

**Finding 8's repair closes the course's own arc.** Lesson 10 now ends its practice with the audit
lesson 1 promised, and says what the number means: if the gap between the level given and the level
deserved has not moved, you have read about reading rather than changed how you read.
