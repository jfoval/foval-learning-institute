# Reviews: The Bible, What It Is and How to Read It — 12-method-tools-plan

Append-only. Newest at the bottom.

## Lesson 12, "A way of reading you can keep" — Stage 4 round 1, 2026-09-08

Three fresh-context subagents (depth and sourcing, fact-check, voice and media with pedagogy).
All applied. Every one of the 21 URLs the lesson recommends was fetched and returns 200, which for a
lesson whose whole purpose is to send a reader to tools is the check that mattered most.

### BLOCKING, applied

- **Augustine was quoted saying what his correspondent said.** The sentence about pagans laughing at
  the Jonah story is the question put to him, not his reply. His actual answer, that either every
  miracle is incredible or there is no reason to disbelieve this one, is now what the lesson reports.
- **The lesson justified daily reading with the spacing literature, which does not support it.**
  Cepeda and colleagues measured distributed re-study of the same material; a linear reading plan is
  new material every day. The mechanism is now stated honestly, and the research is cited for the two
  places it does bear on: M'Cheyne's calendar, which really is spaced re-reading, and the log's demand
  for one observation a sitting, which is retrieval practice.
- **It launched a course project that did not exist.** Both assessments are now written, and the
  project follows the outline's spec and rubric.
- **Philippians 4:13 was quoted in the ESV's wording**, untagged, in a course that quotes the NET by
  design and may not quote the ESV at all under its licence. Corrected.
- **Two prompts printed their answers in plain prose**, which the build had been flagging as a draft
  warning. Both are now predict blocks.
- **Both charts failed.** The circles diagram had seven labels at 10 and 11 units; the reading-time
  chart had ten more inheriting 10 from a group wrapper, two labels hardcoded white, and two of its
  five bars drawn one pixel wide, which its own footnote admitted. Redrawn: nested frames for the
  first, two panels with their own stated scales for the second.
- **The Society for Old Testament Study was quoted against its own conclusion.** Its "too long to be
  a parable" sentence sits inside a paragraph that opens "Granted that the book is not a historical
  narrative", and the lesson used the quotation to support a conservative reply while omitting that
  premise and the reasons given for it. All of it is now on the page.

### Facts corrected

- "Four hundred and fifty years apart" is about 495; Nehemiah 8 is 445 BC and Acts 17 about AD 50.
- Philemon is one of a few letters occasioned from the writer's side, not the only one. The lesson's
  own footnote and lesson 7 both said "few"; only the body said "one".
- Nehemiah 8:7 has the Levites teaching while the people stood, not moving through the crowd.
- Two M'Cheyne quotations were attached to the wrong numbered advantages, and the "green pasture"
  phrase is from his preamble rather than his list.
- The New Testament in a year is about three minutes a day on Crossway's own rate, not six.
- The free NET reader is netbible.org; netbible.com is the publisher's shop.
- The DTS course does not state a duration and requires a sign-up, both of which the lesson now says.
- The course's own translation says Babylonians at Habakkuk 1:6, not Chaldeans.
- "Reading alone is not the ideal the Bible sets" was unsourced and is contradicted by Acts 8. Replaced
  with the sourceable claim about literacy and the cost of copies.
- `minutes: 45` understated the lesson by roughly a factor of three once its own reading assignments
  are counted. Now 150.

### Pedagogy and voice

- Twelve of 56 body paragraphs closed on a one-line moral, and five of the twelve were in the last
  three sections, which is the sermon register arriving exactly where a closing lesson is most at risk.
  Seven rewritten, including the motivational send-off and the four-beat closing listicle.
- Two stretches of 1,900 and 1,300 words asked nothing, the second covering all four misconceptions.
  Two checkpoints added, one of which turns the tools list from a resource roundup into a matching
  exercise, since objective 3 is about matching tool to question and nothing tested it.
- The tools list had one generic entry, and it was the one carrying two of the four questions, plus a
  claim about what four named study Bibles say at three passages, sourced to nothing. Narrowed.
- Quiz: correct answer was the longest option in three of six, one distractor was the silly kind 4.3
  forbids, and only half the items applied to unseen cases. Four items replaced.
- The 3.3 MB Wikimedia original swapped for the 148 KB thumbnail, and the three unlinked reading plans
  and four unlinked sources are now linked where they are named.

### Deliberately not fixed

- The suggestion to work an unworked book in the exercise's part 1 instead of Jonah was not taken.
  Jonah is the lesson's second worked example on purpose, and the project immediately after it sets the
  reader on a book the lesson does not work, with a note steering them away from the two it does. The
  transfer test is met by the project rather than by the exercise, which is the right place for it.
- The proposed chart of the four plans' daily cost was not added. The plans section is a decision, and
  four italic-lead paragraphs with the daily cost in each is enough; another chart would be the third
  in one lesson.

### Still owed, recorded rather than fixed

- Fee and Stuart's *Book by Book* is characterised in the body from the publisher's description. The
  claim is now hedged, but the honest fix is for someone to open the book, since its four headings are
  the pattern the project asks learners to use.
