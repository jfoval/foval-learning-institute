# Reviews: The Bible, What It Is and How to Read It — 09-text-and-reliability

Append-only. Newest at the bottom.

## Lesson 9, "How the text reached us, and how reliable it is" — Stage 4 round 1, 2026-09-08

Five fresh-context subagents: depth, fact-check, neutrality, voice and media, pedagogy. All five
returned. Every finding below was applied in the same session unless marked otherwise. The lesson
was not held for review; it is fixed and stays live-eligible.

### BLOCKING, applied

- **The timeline chart misdrew its own data.** Three reviewers caught it independently. The axis is
  linear at 0.2 px per year, and on that scale Isaiah's composition bar ran 700 to 125 BC against a
  label reading "8th to 6th c. BC", and Paul's ran AD 50 to 200 against a label reading "letters, AD
  50s". The bars had been stretched to touch their manuscript dots, which inverts the chart's whole
  argument by shrinking the gap it exists to show. The chart was redrawn from scratch at a larger
  scale: correct bar geometry, and every label at 15px or above, which also cleared the validator's
  standing 4.6 warning on this lesson.
- **The 400,000 figure was attributed to Ehrman as well as Wallace, and to sources that do not
  carry it.** Neither footnoted page gives a total. The figure is Wallace's, from his 2013 post "The
  Number of Textual Variants: An Evangelical Miscalculation", now fetched, quoted and cited as note
  29. Ehrman's own published estimate is a range, 200,000 to 400,000. Body and quiz both rewritten.
- **Ehrman's concession was quoted with his own qualifier removed.** The blog sentence ends "(at
  least the variations that we know about)". Trimming a sceptic's hedge off the concession the
  lesson leans hardest on is exactly what 3.2 and 3.7 forbid. The parenthesis is restored and the
  predict block now says the parenthesis is his and belongs in the quotation.
- **Ehrman's examples were given in their weakest form.** The lesson had "whether Luke's voice at
  the baptism says 'today I have begotten you'" where Ehrman's claim is that the variant bears on
  whether Luke's Jesus is God's Son eternally or becomes so at his baptism, and "whether Luke's
  Gospel includes the words about the body given for you" where his claim is that on the shorter
  reading Luke has no doctrine of atonement at the Last Supper at all. Both are now stated at full
  strength with verse references, which is what 3.7 requires and what the lesson's own credibility
  move depends on.
- **"No article of the creeds depends on a disputed reading" was filed under history.** It is a
  judgement about what the readings amount to, not a measurement. It now has its own paragraph,
  labelled as a judgement, with Ehrman's circularity caveat and the three variants critics actually
  point to (1 Timothy 3:16, John 1:18, Hebrews 2:9) named so a reader can look them up, followed by
  Wallace's reply.
- **The quiz had zero application items and was guessable by shape.** The correct answer was the
  longest option in five of six and carried a hedge in five of six; all six correct answers restated
  a sentence from the body, and one restated a checkpoint's hidden answer. The Old Testament half of
  the lesson, about 40 percent of the body, was tested by nothing. The quiz is now eight items, four
  of them applying the method to cases the lesson does not work (an unseen Old Testament footnote, a
  harder-reading decision, a classical text with three manuscripts, and Luke 22:43–44), with the two
  strongest original items kept.
- **Sourcing that repeats a defect this course has escalated three times.** Tov was characterised
  and paraphrased, including a claim about the shape of his chapters, though the research file never
  records the book being opened; the same for Metzger on the creeds. Both are now attributed to what
  was actually read, and note 2 says plainly that the book has not been opened here.

### Facts corrected

- "Nineteen centuries of copying by hand" is wrong: hand-copying ended with print. Now fourteen
  centuries of copying and four of printing.
- Vaticanus is not a complete Greek Bible (it lacks the Pastorals, Philemon and Revelation, and
  breaks off at Hebrews 9:14). The quiz option now reads "the two oldest Greek manuscripts of the
  Gospels", which is also Metzger's own wording.
- Sinaiticus does not leave a notable blank after Mark 16:8; the anomalous blank column is
  Vaticanus's. As written the quiz explanation handed a KJV-only reader a real error to catch.
- The Aleppo Codex's losses are not a settled 1947 fire. Roughly 40 percent of the leaves are
  missing, no evidence of fire has been found, and when they disappeared is argued. Said that way now.
- "Every modern Old Testament translation rests on the Leningrad Codex" is overstated twice over: a
  translation is made from BHS or BHQ, editions based on Leningrad, and the Hebrew University Bible
  Project and Jerusalem Crown use Aleppo. Body and figure caption both corrected.
- P52 is asserted as "the oldest" and then undercut two sentences later by Nongbri's redating. Now
  "the one usually called the oldest".
- The 5,800 manuscript count has no reachable INTF source; published totals range from about 5,600.
  Given as a range, with the claim resting on Wallace's quoted words and the register linked.
- Mark 1:41 is not in the blog post it was cited to; it is from *Misquoting Jesus* chapter 5, now
  note 30.
- The Isaiah Scroll is seventeen sheets of prepared skin, about seven metres, not "leather".
- "No scholar of any stripe dates them more than about seventy years after the crucifixion" is an
  absolute that fails on contact with mainstream dates for John. Replaced with the actual ranges.
- Three quotations were tidied silently (a dropped "Yet, even", an inserted "and", a truncated "of
  the evangelicals"). All restored.
- Ketef Hinnom now cites Barkay, Lundberg, Vaughn and Zuckerman, *BASOR* 334 (2004), rather than
  "the standard excavation reports", which named nothing.

### Neutrality (standpoint: christian, standards 3.7)

Perspectives checked: critical text criticism (Ehrman, Parker, Epp, the Münster initial-text
position), thoroughgoing eclecticism (Kilpatrick, Elliott), Byzantine priority (Robinson, Pierpont,
Hodges), conservative evangelical (Wallace, Hurtado, Kruger), mainline (Metzger), Hebrew Bible
scholarship (Tov, Ulrich), Catholic, Orthodox and Jewish.

- Metzger was called "the field's most respected conservative", which he was not; he was a mainline
  Presbyterian who did not hold inerrancy, and the label made a centre-to-centre agreement look like
  a convergence across the ideological range. Relabelled throughout, along with "the critic" for
  Ehrman, who describes himself as a historian and an agnostic.
- Reasoned eclecticism was taught as simply how the field works. Its two live rivals are now named
  in the body, with Byzantine priority given in the form its serious advocates hold rather than the
  vote-counting version that appears as a quiz distractor, and the course's own position stated.
- The textual plurality of the Hebrew Bible was understated to the point of changing what it shows.
  "Some copies of Jeremiah and Samuel match the Septuagint" is now the actual case: a Jeremiah about
  an eighth shorter with the oracles in a different place, which is two editions of a book, plus the
  1 Samuel 17 to 18 effect a reader can see in their own Bible.
- The Septuagint was treated as a witness without saying that this is a Protestant and Catholic
  decision. The Orthodox position is now stated in its own terms.
- A straw sceptic ("a sceptic who says only the third is hiding more") closed the section that had
  just quoted Ehrman saying all three things. Rewritten symmetrically.
- The predict block framed Ehrman's concession as a reveal. It now asks for both halves of his
  position, which is what the standard means by engaging an objection at full strength.
- Also fixed: "late and thin" of the Textus Receptus, "bad arguments about which Bible to trust",
  "not a fourth-century invention" where the hypothesis was a recension, and the "hasn't read them"
  jab, which cut both ways but was still a jab at persons.

### Pedagogy

- The lesson worked three passages and never gave the learner a procedure for a fourth. A six-question
  procedure now sits before the worked examples, followed by the vocabulary of the notes themselves
  (what "some manuscripts" means against "the earliest manuscripts", single brackets, double
  brackets, a bare footnote) and a route to the NET's translators' notes, which the course's own
  research file has recorded as the best free tool for this and which the lesson had never mentioned.
- The harder-reading criterion, the most transferable idea in the field, appeared once, in a
  subordinate clause, inside a checkpoint body the site hides behind a button. Promoted into the
  mechanism section and now tested.
- Objectives rewritten: the first bundled four assessables behind one verb, and the fourth asked for
  something no multiple-choice item could test. There are now five, including one that names the
  transfer skill the new exercise and quiz item test.
- Exercise item 3 asked the reader to look and remember, which is not practice; it now asks for a
  count and two sentences. A new item 4 runs the method on a passage the lesson does not work.
- A misconception ("Sinaiticus and Vaticanus are the Bible") told the reader that sometimes other
  witnesses outweigh the codices and never showed a case. It now points at one the reader has read.

### Voice and media

- Two banned sentence shapes at the top: the "by the end you'll be able to" promise, deleted, and
  four stacked rhetorical questions, cut to one. Also "Let's clear away" and a sentence whose whole
  job was to announce the next sentence.
- Seven paragraph-closing one-line morals, four of them the same paired-symmetry move. Two kept, the
  rest cut or flattened.
- The 78-word summary sentence, four semicolon-joined clauses hanging off one verb, split into four.
- The body had two links in it. Nine primary sources are free online and were verified: Wallace's
  interview, Ehrman's post, all three of Metzger's entries at bible-researcher.com, the Münster
  register, the IAA archive, Vaticanus at the Vatican Library. All are now linked at the point of
  use, which is what 4.5 asks for.
- Two images added, both public domain and both verified: the end of Mark in Vaticanus, which is the
  one picture the lesson kept asking the reader to imagine, and Codex Montfortianus, the manuscript
  produced when Erasmus was challenged over the Comma.
- The Israel Museum's Digital Dead Sea Scrolls host did not resolve; the exercise now sends readers
  to the IAA's Leon Levy library, which does and which is already a source.
- Section heading "a thousand years in one afternoon" promised something the section does not do.
  Now "a thousand-year gap, closed in 1947".

### Unverified, and said so rather than passed

- Metzger and Ehrman, *The Text of the New Testament*, 4th ed.: not available online, no page
  numbers anywhere, not one claim from it verified. The lesson no longer rests anything load-bearing
  on it and note 2 says so.
- The NA28 and UBS5 double brackets at Mark 16:9–20 and John 7:53–8:11: near-certain, universally
  reported, neither edition openable.
- The P75-and-Vaticanus finding: standard, primary study not fetched.
- The blank space after Mark 16:8 in Vaticanus is standard scholarship but was not verified from the
  image at its available resolution. The caption states it; if a higher-resolution folio from
  digi.vatlib.it can be sourced, prefer it.

  The two halves serve one argument and the quiz now covers both; splitting would make lesson 9 the
  only lesson in the course whose halves cannot be read without each other.
- The checkpoint that poses a weighing problem before the first fully worked weighing stays where it
  is. The mechanism is stated immediately above it and the block reveals its own answer, so it is
  doing desirable-difficulty work rather than testing what has not been taught.
