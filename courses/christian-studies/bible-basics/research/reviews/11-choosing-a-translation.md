## Lesson 11, "Choosing a translation" — Stage 4 round 1, 2026-09-08

Four fresh-context subagents (depth and sourcing, fact-check, neutrality, voice and media with pedagogy),
plus a targeted Stage 1 research task to close gaps the reviews found. All applied in the same session.

### BLOCKING, applied

- **Three positions were described in their opponents' words**, which is the 3.7 failure this course
  keeps having to fix. The worst was that **Byzantine or Majority Text priority was run together with
  Received Text priority**, and the merged position was attributed to Wilbur Pickering, who argues
  Majority Text and is a critic of the Received Text. Both facts were sourced to Carson and White,
  two books written against the position and recorded in SOURCES.md as never opened. The lesson now
  has a section on Byzantine priority in its own advocates' words, from Robinson's 2001 essay and
  Hodges's 1968 and 1978 articles, all read: Robinson's "the Byzantine Textform is not the TR, nor
  need it be associated with the TR or those defending such in any manner", his description of the
  method as "reasoned transmissionalism", and his own rejection of the nose-counting caricature.
  The gap between the two texts is now quantified from three independent counts (Robinson's edition
  says over 1,800 and his collation lists 1,883; Wallace counted 1,838; Pickering predicts over
  1,500), and Hodges is quoted announcing that his edition deletes the Comma Johanneum and Acts
  8:37, which is the single fact that makes the distinction impossible to miss.
- **The complementarian case on gender language was a preface and three assertions.** The inclusive
  side got its corpus research and the other side got adjectives. The lesson now carries the argument
  its authors actually make: Poythress and Grudem's pure-generic against representative-generic
  distinction, their explicit denial that they are arguing for formal equivalence, Revelation 3:20
  and Psalm 1 as their worked cases, Grudem's published tallies, the 1997 Colorado Springs Guidelines
  with their signatories and the International Bible Society's later withdrawal, Strauss's reply that
  translation follows sense rather than form, the translation committee's own 2010 notes conceding two
  of the three main points, and the 2011 counts from both sides. All from sources read this session.
- **Catholic and Orthodox translation was absent**, in a lesson that then recommended eight Bibles,
  every one of them a sixty-six book Protestant edition, to a reader lesson 8 had taught that the
  Catholic canon has seventy-three. There is now a section on the Catholic and Orthodox shelf, and the
  Septuagint question is named where the source texts are introduced rather than left as a silent
  default. *Liturgiam Authenticam* is quoted correctly, since it is usually cited for the opposite of
  what it says: new translations "must be made directly from the original texts".
- **The strongest King James Only position is still owed.** It is named in the lesson, distinguished
  from the Trinitarian Bible Society and Dean Burgon Society positions, and the lesson says where to
  press it. What it does not yet have is a primary statement of the view in its own advocates' words,
  because the research for it was not in SOURCES.md and the pass to get it did not land in time. The
  lesson now says plainly that the course has not yet read a primary advocate of that position rather
  than summarising it from its critics, which is the honest interim state. **This is the one open item
  on the lesson.**
- **The chart carried 25 sub-legible labels and hardcoded gradient colours.** Nine versions packed
  into 380 viewBox units cannot hold legible type, so the layout is now vertical, in three bands, with
  every font-size on its own element and every colour tokenised.
- **Four links in 9,650 words**, none of them at the point where a document is quoted, in a lesson
  built entirely on quoting prefaces. Every preface it quotes is free online and is now linked where
  it is quoted.

### Facts corrected

- **The NIV preface does not say what was attributed to it.** The lesson credited it with the
  reasoning behind rendering *adelphoi* "brothers and sisters"; the words *adelphoi* and *brothers*
  do not occur in the preface, and the research file had already recorded that the committee's
  reasoning was seen only in secondary summaries. Reattributed, with the Collins figure kept because
  it is in the preface and correct.
- Erasmus's 1516 was the first printed Greek New Testament **to be published**; the Complutensian was
  printed two years earlier and held back. His own line about the edition is now quoted from Allen's
  edition of the letter, addressed to Nikolaus Ellenbog, about April 1516, with the note that the
  widely circulated Latin form is not his wording and that the second half of his sentence, a boast,
  is usually dropped.
- The Elzevirs printed the 1633 edition; Daniel Heinsius wrote the preface with the famous sentence.
- The NKJV note at Mark 16:9 does not begin "NU-Text"; it says the verses are bracketed in NU.
- Two ESV preface quotations were trimmed of the clauses that carry their argument. Restored, and the
  research file corrected, since the truncation was inherited from it.
- Amos 4:6 in the NET begins "But surely", which the lesson had dropped.
- "The Trinitarian Bible Society has published only the Authorised Version since its founding" could
  not be sourced; narrowed to what is verifiable.
- A quiz explanation had the critical editions built "from thousands of earlier" manuscripts, which
  overstates the early evidence in exactly the way this course warns against.

### Neutrality

Perspectives checked: Received Text priority in its two organised forms, Byzantine priority,
KJV-as-preserved-English, formal equivalence, functional equivalence, both sides of the gender
dispute, Catholic, Orthodox, and conservative Hebraists on *almah*.

- **The lesson worked the Received Text position's weakest case and none of its strongest.** 1 John
  5:7 got a whole section; 1 Timothy 3:16 and Mark 1:2, which is where its defenders think they are
  strongest, were absent. Both are now given, with the harder-reading answer, and the section says
  explicitly that the Comma is the case with least manuscript support and so not a fair sample.
- **The trade was framed throughout in the formal camp's terms**, as accuracy against readability.
  The functional camp's own claim, that meaning-based translation is the more accurate method, is now
  stated where the spectrum is introduced.
- **The publishers' own names for their own methods were all in the footnotes and none in the body.**
  "Essentially literal", "complete equivalence", "optimal equivalence", "as literal as possible, as
  free as necessary" are now in the text, with the observation that three of them are claiming the
  spectrum is a false choice.
- *almah* at Isaiah 7:14 was presented as settled on one side, with a single translation's note as
  support. It is now given as argued, with the conservative Hebraists' case stated.
- "Not a conspiracy" removed from the Connections summary: nobody in that camp describes their
  position that way, and the word belongs to their critics.
- "Paraphrase" was used as a category verdict. The lesson now distinguishes a paraphrase proper,
  which reworks an existing English version, from a free translation made from the original languages,
  and notes that Peterson resisted the label for that reason.

### Pedagogy and voice

- Four blocks in the first 1,600 words and one in the last 3,500. The 2,359-word stretch covering the
  hardest material asked nothing, and the one retrieval prompt inside it printed its answer in plain
  prose. That prompt is now a predict block, and a checkpoint was added after the gender section.
- Thirteen of 55 body paragraphs closed on a one-line moral, clustered three in a row. Six cut.
- The banned "by the end of this lesson" promise, and the same three-question routine performed twice
  56 lines apart. Both fixed.
- Three sentences over 100 words split, and a 127-word run of undated-purpose dates cut to the three
  that teach something, since the chart already carries them all.
- The lesson promised the reader could identify any Bible's text and method and never said where to
  look. There is now a short section on the preface, the copyright page and the footnote conventions.
- The reading-levels paragraph told the reader to treat as a hint a number the lesson had just refused
  to give. Replaced with a test they can run in two minutes.
- Quiz: three of five correct answers were the longest option and four of five were the only balanced
  one, so a test-wise reader scored full marks by shape. Three items replaced, and the quiz now runs
  to six with four applying to cases the lesson does not work.
