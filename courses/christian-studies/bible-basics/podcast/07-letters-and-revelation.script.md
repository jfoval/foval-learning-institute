S2: So what is it?

S1: Fee calls it a unique, finely blended combination of three distinct literary types. Apocalypse, prophecy, and letter. It's a letter: it opens to the seven churches of Asia, so it was occasioned by real churches with real trouble. It's prophecy: John calls it that, which means a word from God into a present situation, to churches under persecution from without and decay from within. And it's an apocalypse, the genre we have no modern equivalent for.

S2: And how does that change the reading?

S1: It runs the letter's problem backwards. A letter's meaning sits partly off the page, so a reader who refuses to reconstruct gets less than the writer meant. An apocalypse's sits in the picture first, so a reader who decodes the picture into events has changed the genre. Fee says the visions work like parables. See each one as a whole, because the whole vision is trying to say something, and treat the details as either dramatic effect or as filling in the picture. The sun turning black and the stars falling like figs probably don't mean anything one by one. They make the earthquake more terrible.

S2: And the numbers?

S1: Symbols. Seven churches, seven seals, seven trumpets, seven bowls. Twelve tribes and twelve apostles multiplied into a hundred and forty four thousand. Forty two months. Most commentators read that hundred and forty four thousand as twelve times twelve times a thousand rather than a headcount, because every other number in the book is symbolic. Futurists who take it as a count are being consistent with their own rule rather than careless with ours.

S2: What else?

S1: The images come from the Old Testament. On Fee's count John cites or echoes it over two hundred and fifty times, so that every significant moment in his narrative is imaged almost exclusively in Old Testament language. The first readers heard the echoes at once. We look them up.

S2: Where do I start?

S1: With the images John decodes himself. The one like a son of man is Christ. The lampstands are the churches. The dragon is Satan. The prostitute is the great city that has sovereignty over the kings of the earth, which Fee and most commentators take to be Rome. Start from what the author decoded before you decode anything else.
